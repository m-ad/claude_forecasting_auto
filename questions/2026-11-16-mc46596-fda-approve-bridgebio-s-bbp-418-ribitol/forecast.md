### Outside view

Reference class: novel NDAs/BLAs at CDER under priority review with orphan designation and no advisory committee. CDER first-cycle approval for novel drugs was 74% in 2024 and 85% in 2025 [5]; priority-review NMEs have run >80% since FY2011 [5]. But that is approval *in the first cycle*, not approval *by the PDUFA date*: 3-month major-amendment extensions are common in 2026 (Savara, Beren, Agios, Capricor) [6], and the question text notes 3 of 18 remaining Q4 2026 decisions had already been pushed back. Starting point ≈ 80% for "approved on or before the goal date", conditional on no extension having been announced 11 days out (which removes some extension risk — most are announced weeks earlier).

### Inside view

- **For (+5 pp):** FORTIFY's 12-month interim met the primary endpoint (glycosylated α-dystroglycan, 1.8× baseline) and functional secondaries: 100-metre timed test difference ~0.27 m/s and FVC difference 5% predicted (p=.007); CK −82%; adverse events similar to placebo [2][4]. The FDA itself recommended traditional approval and saw no need for an advisory committee [1]. No reported FDA concerns; BridgeBio said on 2026-10-29 it is "weeks away" with field team deployed [3]. Unmet need is total (no approved LGMD therapy).
- **Against (−5 pp):** The 2026 FDA has issued CRLs to rare-disease filings resting on unvalidated surrogates (bitopertin, RGX-121) [7], and leadership is sceptical of single-trial-plus-surrogate packages; the filing rests on an interim of an ongoing Phase 3. Unlike those, BBP-418 has statistically significant functional benefit, which mitigates most of this. Residual risks: late major-amendment extension (~4%), CRL (~7%), FDA missing the date / slight slip (~3%).
- Net ≈ 85%. Markets (Kalshi ~89–91%, thin) and Jefferies (>90%) sit a bit higher; BiopharmaWatch ~78% lower. I shade below the market because the window ends exactly on the goal date.
