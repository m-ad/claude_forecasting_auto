## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-20

### What resolves YES

YES if the final electoral roll for Andhra Pradesh from the 2026 Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is officially published on **29 or 30 October 2026, India Standard Time (IST)**. "Published" means an Election Commission of India (ECI) or Andhra Pradesh Chief Electoral Officer (CEO) note announcing it, a downloadable "SIR FinalRoll - 2026" on voters.eci.gov.in, or credible media reporting the publication and its date. NO if the date is extended past 30 October, or if nothing is out by 23:59 IST on 30 October. Publication before 29 October also resolves NO, but the claims-disposal deadline of 22 October makes that very unlikely. Draft or supplementary rolls do not count. A final roll published with some cases still pending (as in West Bengal in 2026) would still count.

### Outside view

There are two reference classes, and they point in different directions.

1. **Did a scheduled SIR final-roll date hold?** For Andhra Pradesh, never so far. It has slipped four times: ~22 Sep → 3 Oct → 9 Oct → 16 Oct → 30 Oct [1][2][3]. Phase 3 as a whole has mostly slipped. Telangana was extended three times, Maharashtra by 28 days, and Karnataka/Delhi by 20 days [4][5]. Uttar Pradesh in phase 2 was extended four times [7]. On this class, the chance that the current date holds is perhaps 40–55%.
2. **When the last-set date arrives, is the roll published on it?** In every confirmed case, yes. Bihar (30 Sep 2025), Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, West Bengal (28 Feb), UP (10 Apr), Odisha (21 Sep, revised), Punjab (12 Oct 2026, after an 11-day extension), Manipur and Sikkim all published on the final date. The scout found no early or late publications [6][7][8]. So the risk sits almost entirely in whether there is another extension. A missed date with no new order does not happen.

### Inside view

Evidence for the 30 October date holding:
- **Backlog.** Notice hearings were 99.9% done (44.27 of 44.28 lakh; 1 lakh = 100,000) by about 30 Sep [9]. The remaining work was the Form 6/7/8 queue: about 69k, 8.5k and 32k pending [9]. The extension that followed was the longest yet at 14 days. It leaves 8 days between the 22 Oct disposal cut-off and publication, a larger buffer than before.
- **Pressure to finish.** On 9 Oct the Supreme Court said "SIR cannot be a ground to delay" Andhra Pradesh local-body polls, which the High Court wants started within about six weeks of 1 Oct [10]. The State Election Commissioner said the new roll will be used only if the SIR is final when polls are notified [11]. That gives the state an incentive to finish. It does not compel the ECI.
- **Peers.** Punjab published on its revised date [8]. Meghalaya, extended in the same 30 Sep order as AP, was "preparing" for 26 Oct, with 95% of hearings done by 12–13 Oct [12].

Evidence against:
- **Track record.** Every previous AP date was extended, often only 2–14 days before it fell due [2][3]. With 10 days left, a fifth order cannot be ruled out. The ECI did not give a reason for the 14 Oct extension [1], so I cannot tell whether the backlog that caused it has cleared.
- **No fresh signal.** Neither I nor the scout found any CEO statement of readiness, or any reporting after 15 Oct.
- **Internal dissent.** Two Election Commissioners have objected to Form 6 and roll-software decisions [13]. I see no direct link to AP's schedule, but it is a source of friction.
- **Peers keep slipping.** Haryana and AP originally shared the 22 Sep date. Haryana has since moved to 6 Nov, with 28,000 hearings pending on 7 Oct. Maharashtra was extended a second time, to 16 Nov (scout 3, UNI / newsonair). Repeat extensions are normal in phase 3.

Estimate: P(no further extension) ≈ 0.58. P(published on 29–30 Oct IST | no extension) ≈ 0.95. This allows for a release dated after midnight IST, or a resolver finding no report. That gives **≈ 0.55**.

### Evidence

- 14 Oct 2026: an ECI letter (under secretary Sandeep Kumar) moves AP final publication from 16 Oct to 30 Oct, and claims disposal from 10 Oct to 22 Oct. It gives no reason [1].
- 30 Sep 2026: AP moved from 9 Oct to 16 Oct, Meghalaya from 21 Oct to 26 Oct. The stated reasons were pending no-mapping and logical-discrepancy hearings and pending Form 6/7/8 applications [2][3].
- 21 Sep: Form 6 pendency was 1,76,915 [scout 1, Deccan Chronicle]. About 30 Sep it was 69,184 of 4.04 lakh [9].
- 9 Oct: the Supreme Court said local polls will go ahead on the old rolls if the SIR is not final by notification [10].
- 12 Oct: Punjab's final roll was published on its revised date [8].
- Phase 2 and phase 3 history: see the outside view; per-state table from scout 2 [6][7].

### What would change my mind

- A new ECI communique moving the date past 30 Oct. Given the pattern, one would most likely come around 22–28 Oct. That would mean NO.
- A CEO AP statement that disposal is complete by 22 Oct, or a published date for the local-body poll notification. Either would push me towards 75–80%.
