## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-18

### What resolves YES

The option naming the party with the most seats in the 120th Congress (elected 3 Nov 2026, seated 3 Jan 2027). Independents count with the party they caucus with. "Other" resolves only on an exact R–D tie, a third party or non-caucusing independents leading, or vacancies exceeding both parties' seats. Special elections, recounts and run-offs are included.

### Outside view

The president's party has lost House seats in 18 of 20 midterms since 1946 (roughly 90%), and nearly always when approval is below 50%. The starting majority is about 218R–215D, so a small net Democratic gain flips control. Redistricting since 2025 shifted a few seats toward Republicans, but a 5-11 point Democratic generic-ballot lead still exceeds that. Base rate for a flip in this configuration is ~80-90%.

### Inside view

Markets and models both put Democrats well ahead. I follow the markets but trim slightly: the figures I found are 2-13 days old, and markets on a clear favourite can be overconfident. "Other" is nearly impossible: an exact tie or a third-party plurality is about 0.2-0.5%. No quantitative model of my own was run.

### Evidence

- Kalshi priced Democratic House control at about 90% on 16 Oct 2026 (NYT collection, via search summary) [2].
- A DeFi Rate average of Kalshi (91.5%) and Polymarket (92.5%) gave Democrats about 92% on 5 Oct 2026 [1].
- A 270toWin piece of 24 Sep 2026 says quantitative forecasts lean toward larger Democratic gains; generic-ballot averages show Democrats ahead by roughly 5-11 points [3].
- Rating changes by Cook, Sabato and Inside Elections, Jan to mid-July 2026: 92 of 113 favoured Democrats [2].
- A Decision Desk HQ model in August had Democrats at about 62-63%; older and lower, possibly stale [2].
- Not checked: individual poll releases and the current redistricting litigation status.
