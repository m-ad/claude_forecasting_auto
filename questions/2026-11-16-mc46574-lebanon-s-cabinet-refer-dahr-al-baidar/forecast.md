## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-16

### What resolves YES

YES if Lebanon's Council of Ministers decides, before 28 November 2026 (Beirut time), to refer the Dahr al-Baidar explosives case to the Judicial Council (al-Majlis al-'Adli), as reported by Naharnet or other credible sources. A joint referral with the al-Qasr file counts, and so does an approval in principle announced by the information minister. A session on 27 November counts even if it is reported later. A postponement, deferral or withdrawal is NO. So is a transfer by the military judiciary or the Justice Ministry alone. Arithmetic: from 16 November, the usual Thursday/Friday cadence leaves about **two sessions** (around 19–20 and 26–27 November), and sessions are often cancelled or limited to one topic. The request has been pending since about 21 October. It was deferred on 22 October, kept off the agenda on 29 October, and kept off the electricity-only session on 13 November: so 0 for 3 sessions so far.

### Outside view

Reference class: a contested Lebanese cabinet decision that has already been deferred for about 3–4 weeks, opposed by the Shiite duo (Hezbollah and Amal), and given a "soon on the agenda" signal from the president and PM. Hard data on this class does not exist. Lebanese cabinets routinely keep such files "pending" for months; the Tayouneh and port-blast fights of 2021 paralysed the government. Counter-examples: the cabinet passed the August 2025 arms-monopoly decisions over a Shiite walkout, and, by Salam's own account, earlier security cases were referred within 1–2 days. My prior that a stalled file is decided within two sessions is about **15–20 %**.

### Inside view

- **For YES (+15 pp):** On 13 November, MTV, reported by Naharnet [1], said Aoun and Salam had concluded the referral should "soon" go on an upcoming agenda. IMLebanon [2] reported a proposal to put it to a vote at next week's session. The Lebanese Forces boycott (three of four ministers still out [5]), US pressure, and the military prosecutor's charges against 13 people on 30 October all raise the cost of further delay. The charges give a legal hook for a referral. A simple majority is plausibly enough, and Hezbollah and Amal hold about 5 of 24 seats.
- **For NO (−5 pp):** The 22 October deferral was brokered by Aoun, Berri and Salam [3][4]. Berri reportedly asked the Saudi envoy to lean on Salam [2]. A ministerial source said the referral stays postponed until the investigation ends [4], and the military-court track is moving (a hearing is set for 17 November). Shiite ministers reportedly threatened a walkout if it came to a vote. "On the agenda" is not "approved": a Lebanese-style outcome is another deferral, or a compromise that leaves the file with the military court. Only two sessions remain, and either can slip.

The net is about **30 %**, with the IQR 20–40 %. The forecast is most sensitive to whether the item actually appears on the 19–20 November agenda.

### Evidence

- 2026-10-22: the cabinet deferred the referral. It chose to wait for the investigation, and the LF ministers walked out [3][4].
- 2026-10-29: the LF boycotted the waste-management session. The referral was not on the agenda [3].
- 2026-10-30/31: the military prosecutor charged 13 people [6].
- 2026-11-13: Aoun and Salam decided to put the referral on the agenda "soon" [1]. Salam refused to raise it in the electricity-only session the same day [2]. Saddi returned to cabinet, while the other three LF ministers continue the boycott [5].
- I found no published agenda for the next session, searched 2026-11-16.

### What would change my mind

- An announced agenda for the ~19–20 November session that includes the referral would move me to about 50 %. A session announced on a single other topic, or a cancellation, would move me toward 15 %.
- Statements from Berri or Hezbollah accepting the referral, or LF ministers returning without one, which would signal a compromise that keeps the file in the military court.
