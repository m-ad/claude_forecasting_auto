## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-22

### What resolves YES

YES only if credible sources report that by 31 October 2026, 23:59 Bolivia time (UTC−4), Fernando Cerimedo has physically left a Bolivian penitentiary. He must no longer be held under preventive detention in *any* of his cases. Ways that count: detention revoked, detention replaced by house arrest, electronic monitoring or another non-custodial measure, or the cases dismissed. These do not count: a prison transfer (for example Chonchocoro → Palmasola), a change in which case grounds his detention, escape, or extradition while in custody. If his status is unclear, the question resolves NO. Because there are at least two independent six-month orders, both would have to be lifted inside a nine-day window.

### Outside view

No clean reference class exists. The closest is high-profile pretrial detainees in Bolivia who are about two months into a 180-day order that an appeals chamber has just confirmed. They rarely get release or house arrest within the next 10 days:

- Bolivia holds 58–70% of its prisoners in pretrial detention [6].
- Cessation hearings are often suspended: in 31% of cases for lack of notification, 29% because judges are overloaded, 13% because the prosecutor does not appear [6].

The status quo strongly favours NO. Over a ~9-day window my rough prior is 2–4%, and that applies to a *single* detention order.

### Inside view

The evidence pushes the forecast down from that prior:

- **Two orders must fall, not one.** The first is 180 days in the attempted-femicide case (Judge Espada, 21 Aug), confirmed on appeal by the Sala Penal Primera of Santa Cruz on 6–7 Oct [1][2]. The second is six months in the illicit-enrichment case (Judge Borda, 29 Aug), also upheld when the Sala Penal Segunda declared the appeal inadmissible [3][4].
- **More cases are open.** A domestic-violence imputation with a detention request (18 Sep) and a drug-trafficking probe in Beni are also live [4][7].
- **Defence challenges have failed.** His acciones de libertad (habeas-corpus-style challenges) were rejected in September [3].
- **The judge cited flight risk.** He was arrested at the airport trying to fly to Argentina, which makes house arrest unlikely.

What pushes the other way:

- The Attorney General who led the cases, Roger Mariaca, was arrested on 1 Oct over alleged narco links. The defence argues he orchestrated the case [5][8]. This is a real lever for a nullity or cessation request.
- His lawyer said on 2 Oct that there are "expectativas de que en noviembre Cerimedo recupere su libertad" [4]. But even taken at face value, that points to *after* the deadline, and no legal basis was given.

I found no scheduled hearing inside the window. Bolivian politics is volatile, and the Paz–Lara feud makes the case political, so I keep a small tail. **Forecast: 3%.**
