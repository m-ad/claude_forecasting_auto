## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-05

### What resolves YES

YES if José Mourinho is **continuously** the manager (head coach) of Real Madrid CF from the
moment the question opens (2026-10-06 17:00 UTC) until **12:00 UTC on 2026-12-31**. There is no
fine print. Any break counts as NO: a sacking, a "mutual agreement" exit, a resignation, or a
move to another job. Edge cases: (a) a touchline ban or a short medical absence with an
assistant in the dugout should not count as a break, because he stays the appointed manager.
The criteria do not say this, so it is my reading. (b) A dismissal announced before Dec 31 but
taking effect later would arguably still be NO under "continuously … until". (c) Pérez's past
dismissals cluster just *after* the window (Benítez 4 Jan 2016, Xabi Alonso 12 Jan 2026), so a
"sacked in early January" path resolves YES. Source: Metaculus question text, retrieved
2026-10-05 via the browser API path [1].

### Outside view

**Reference class A: every Real Madrid season since 2000-01 (n=26).** Did the manager in post on
about 5 October leave before 31 December of the same year? Yes in **4 of 26** seasons: García Remón
(sacked 30 Dec 2004), Luxemburgo (4 Dec 2005), Schuster (9 Dec 2008), Lopetegui (29 Oct 2018) [2].
Base rate **15%** exit, so **85%** stay.

**Reference class B: summer hires in their first season who were still in post in early October
(n=10).** Queiroz 2003, Capello 2006, Schuster 2007, Pellegrini 2009, Mourinho 2010, Ancelotti
2013, Benítez 2015, Lopetegui 2018, Ancelotti 2021, Xabi Alonso 2025. **1 of 10** left before
Dec 31 (Lopetegui) [2]. That gives **90%** stay. Two near-misses (Benítez, Alonso) were sacked within
two weeks *after* Dec 31.

**Reference class C: Mourinho's own record.** He has been dismissed mid-season in Dec 2015
(Chelsea), Dec 2018 (Man Utd), Apr 2021 (Tottenham), Jan 2024 (Roma) and Aug 2025 (Fenerbahçe).
But all of those came in his **second or third season** at the club. He has never been sacked in
the first half of a first season. Rough first-season hazard: low.

Blend: **~86–88%** stay.

### Inside view

The main driver is Florentino Pérez's patience. The forces for and against an early dismissal:

- **For staying (strong):** Mourinho was the centrepiece of Pérez's June 2026 re-election
  campaign. Pérez said he had the "firm intention" to bring him back and won 65% of the vote
  [3]. Firing him within six months would be a direct, personal embarrassment. The contract runs
  to June 2029 and the reported release/severance cost is large [3]. The season record is
  respectable so far: 6W–2L in competitive games, 4th on 15 points after 7 rounds, and a 2–1
  Champions League win over Inter [4][5].
- **For leaving (moderate):** Barcelona lead on 21 points (7/7), six ahead of Madrid [5].
  Since mid-September there have been press reports of a "lost dressing room", defensive
  fragility, and stars (Vinícius, Mbappé) overriding the coach [6][7]. Atlético won the derby
  2–1 on 20 Sep [8]. Pérez has shown he will act fast: Lopetegui went after 14 games, Alonso
  after about 7 months [2].
- **Decision calendar before Dec 31:** Villarreal (home, ~10 Oct), Roma away in the Champions
  League (14 Oct), **Clásico at Camp Nou (25 Oct)** [9], then November league games and Champions
  League matchdays 3–5. Lopetegui was sacked the day after a 5–1 Clásico defeat (Oct 2018). That
  is the template for the main NO path.

Decomposition of NO (≈13%):
- P(a crisis develops: ≥10 points off the top, or a heavy Clásico defeat plus Champions League
  trouble) ≈ 30%
- P(Pérez acts before Dec 31 | crisis) ≈ 35%. He usually waits for the winter break or January.
  That gives ≈ 10.5%.
- Plus a non-results exit (resignation, health, falling-out with the board or players) ≈ 2%.
- Plus sacking without a deep crisis ≈ 0.5%.
- Total NO ≈ **13%**.
