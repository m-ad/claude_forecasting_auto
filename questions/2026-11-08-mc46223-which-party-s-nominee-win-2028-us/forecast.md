## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-08

### What resolves YES

Multiple choice, three options: "Democratic Party", "Republican Party", "Other". It resolves to the party whose candidate wins the 2028 US presidential election, meaning 270+ Electoral College votes per credible media projections, or Congress's certification if there is material dispute, or a House contingent election if nobody reaches 270. **Other** covers two cases: a winner who represents neither major party, or no election held in 2028. Timeline: the question closes 2028-11-06 and the election is 2028-11-07, about 2 years from today (2026-11-08). Trump is barred by the 22nd Amendment, and he has said it is "pretty clear" he cannot run, so this is an open-seat race.

### Outside view

Reference class: elections where one party has held the White House for eight consecutive years and tries for a third term.
- Since 1948, that party has won only once in six or seven tries (1988) [5].
- Over the longer history, FiveThirtyEight puts the in-party's success rate at 36–50% [5].
- I put the in-party (Republican) base rate at about 35–40%, which gives Democrats about 60–65%.
- Third parties last won electoral votes in 1968, and none has ever won the presidency. A cancelled election would be unprecedented.
- I give Other about 2%.

### Inside view

- **Midterms.** Democrats took both chambers on 2026-11-03 in what was widely described as a rout [2][3][4]. Aggregator tallies put them at about 225 House seats and 54 Senate seats; those counts are unverified by AP. Trump's approval was around 33–38% [4]. Commentators, Nate Silver and Ron DeSantis among them, read the result as damage to the Republican brand that will last [4]. Historically a midterm wave against the in-party during a second term has preceded a loss of the White House (2006→2008). Recovery cases do exist, but they involve a sitting president running for re-election (2010→2012), which does not apply here.
- **Markets.** As of 2026-11-08 the real-money markets put Democrats at 66.5% (Kalshi) and 67.5% (Polymarket), up from about 62% before the midterms [1]. Volume is thin, at $7–13K a day.
- **Against a strong Democratic lean.** Two years is a long time: the economy, war and the candidates can all move. No nominee is known yet. Vance–Newsom polls are roughly tied, with Vance slightly ahead in several [6]. The Electoral College tilts slightly Republican. Long-dated markets are also noisy.
- **My adjustments.**
  - Starting point: the pre-midterm blend of base rate and markets, about 62% Democratic.
  - The midterm wave adds about +3 pp.
  - Horizon and candidate uncertainty subtract about 2 pp against the current market.
  - Result: Democratic 65%, Republican 33%, Other 2%. The Other share covers an election not held or disrupted, a non-major-party winner, and resolution oddities.

### What would change my mind

- **Toward Republicans:** a strong Republican nominee who runs clearly apart from Trump (for example Rubio) and leads in polls, a Democratic nominee seen as too far left, or a good economy in 2028.
- **Toward Democrats:** Trump's approval stays under 40% into 2028, or a recession.
- **Toward Other:** credible moves to postpone the election, or a serious third-party candidate (Tucker Carlson has been rumoured).
