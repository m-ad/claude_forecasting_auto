## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-02

### What resolves YES

YES requires a Duma plenary vote adopting in first reading, on a date before 13 Nov 2026 (Moscow time), bill No. 1308850-8 (senators, introduced 5 Aug 2026), No. 5261-9 (deputies Gavrilov and Ten, introduced 6 Oct 2026) or any other bill setting 1 Jan 2027 as the entry-into-force date of the sanatsiya provisions of Law 253-FZ. Rejection, return to the initiator or postponement does not count. Arithmetic: as of 30 Oct 2026 neither bill has a first-reading date (tracker updated 30 Oct). The question's own text says the window effectively contains one plenary week, 10–12 Nov (Tue–Thu); 3–5 Nov may also hold sessions (4 Nov is a public holiday), but I could not verify the calendar. The window therefore holds roughly 3–6 plenary days. Bill 1308850-8 has been pending 89 days, 5261-9 for 27 days, without being scheduled.

### Outside view

No clean reference class. Closest: urgent, "technical" date-shift bills with ministry support. Once the Duma Council schedules them they pass first reading within days, but the time until scheduling is highly variable, and these two have already waited weeks to months. The "will X happen in the next ~10 days when no date has been announced" base rate is low, roughly 15–25%. I found no systematic statistic (n=0 usable; anecdotes only).

### Inside view

For YES: Ministry of Economic Development proposed the same date (15 Sep strategic session with the Prime Minister), Delovaya Rossiya backs it (16 Oct), and the chair of the responsible Duma committee (Gavrilov) co-authored 5261-9. The Samolet restructuring (VTB, 30 Oct) gives a visible reason to hurry, and the change is a one-line date shift. Against: no date is announced, the senators' bill has sat unscheduled since August, and the "preliminary consideration" of 12 Oct (probably the Council sending it for responses, unverified) suggests a feedback period ending around mid-November. The bill also shifts dependent deadlines, so redrafting is possible. Budget work crowds the agenda, and the following plenary week (17–19 Nov) falls outside the window. Samolet's creditors can use the existing Art. 31.1 of 127-FZ, so the date shift is not strictly needed. Net: roughly 27%.
