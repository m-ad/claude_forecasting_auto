## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-19

### What resolves YES

YES only if, at some point from 2026-10-29 00:00 UTC to 2026-10-30 23:59 UTC, official representatives of **both** Russia and Ukraine have publicly confirmed a mutually agreed arrangement (truce, moratorium, corridor, grain/shipping deal, or a general ceasefire covering the whole war) under which both commit to stop attacking commercial shipping and/or port infrastructure in the Black Sea. Credible sources must report it. A deal reached before 29 October counts only if neither side has withdrawn, suspended or voided it by then. Proposals, agreements in principle, unilateral declarations, deals only one side confirms, and energy-only truces do not count. That also rules out a re-run of March 2025, when the US announced a deal and Russia made it conditional on sanctions relief. If reporting conflicts, the preponderance of credible sources decides. Status today (2026-10-19): no agreement is in effect.

### Outside view

The reference class is a Russia–Ukraine Black Sea shipping deal going from "talks under way, Russia rejecting" to "confirmed by both sides and in effect" within about 11 days.
- The only deal that came into force, the 2022 Black Sea Grain Initiative, took about 3 months of Turkey/UN mediation (roughly April to 22 July 2022). Russia had a clear motive: getting its own grain and fertiliser exports unblocked.
- The US-announced arrangement of 25 March 2025 never took effect. The Kremlin made it conditional on sanctions relief (Rosselkhozbank, the Russian state agricultural bank, back on SWIFT, among other demands).
- Since August 2026, Russia has rejected every limited Black Sea truce: Zakharova on 14 August, Putin personally around 23 August, Peskov's "piecemeal arrangements" on 2 October, and another Turkish/UN proposal around 9 October. On 12 October it also rejected the 60-day general ceasefire.

Ceasefire-type deals that close from that position within two weeks are rare. I put the base rate at about 2–4%.

### Inside view

The specifics push down, not up:
1. **Russia's stated position is categorical.** It wants a comprehensive settlement, not partial truces. Lavrov called a return to the 2022 model "completely unacceptable and unrealistic". Peskov said on 2 October that "piecemeal arrangements cannot bring us closer to a peaceful settlement" [3][4].
2. **Ukraine has added a condition.** Sybiha says Kyiv is ready for an energy and Black Sea ceasefire "only in that combination" [4]. A Black Sea-only deal is therefore not on offer either, and the energy truce announced on 11 October collapsed within a day [7].
3. **Fighting at sea continues.** Russia struck a Liberian-flagged freighter in the Odesa region on 3 October, killing one crew member. The Atlantic Council (9 October) reports rising attacks on shipping and ports [5][8].
4. **The upside channel is real but slow.** A Turkey/UN track mediated by the Swiss Centre for Humanitarian Dialogue was set up for early October [2]. US pressure is erratic: a diesel arrangement with Putin, and Trump's "time for a new president" remark. A sudden Trump–Putin package that Kyiv accepts cannot be ruled out. Even then, the criteria need *both* governments to confirm it publicly *and* it to be in effect by 29–30 October. The March 2025 precedent shows that "deal announced" and "deal in effect, confirmed by both" are different things.
5. **Resolver risk is small.** An LLM resolver could misread a US announcement as a bilateral agreement. The fine print explicitly requires confirmation from both parties, which limits this.

Speculation flagged: the chance of a surprise package (sanctions relief traded for a maritime truce) is my judgement, not something any source states. Net: about 2.5%.
