## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-16

### What resolves YES

The stage counts only through public BCP or intervention-team announcements made **on or before 27 Nov 2026 (Paraguay time)**. "At least one recipient institution has been named" wins if the BCP publicly names any specific institution (a bank, finance company, bridge bank or public entity) to receive part of Ueno's assets, liabilities or deposits. Banks that only act as FGD payout or account-opening channels do not count; nor do Ueno Casa de Bolsa or the fund manager. "Tender launched" wins if a call for bids, published bases, or a publicly announced bidding process (restricted processes included) exists and no institution has been named. Otherwise "No tender…". Statements of intent and the finished balance de exclusión do not count. **Arithmetic:** the resolution started 2 Nov, so 27 Nov is **day 25**. Today is 16 Nov, leaving **11 days**. On 12 Nov Schreiner said the balance de exclusión needed about 10 more days, i.e. around 22 Nov, which leaves about 5 days for a tender call and award before the cutoff. Other reporting put the transfer about 15 days after 12 Nov, i.e. around 27 Nov, right on the cutoff.

### Outside view

The closest precedent is Cefisa (2022–23), resolved under the same Ley 2334. The resolution started on 6 Dec 2022. On 6 Jan 2023 (**31 days** later) a board resolution transferred its assets and liabilities to Bancop, Banco Río, Banco Basa and two finance companies. That was a competitive process, but it apparently became public only at the award. Ley 2334 gives 15 days to pay the guarantee, extendable by 15 more. That 30-day ceiling seems to drive the timing, and for Ueno it lands around 2 Dec, just after this question's cutoff. ARA (2015) also ended with a single-bank transfer negotiated by the intervenors; I found no exact dates. Ueno is far larger (USD 2.8 bn, ~3 m clients), and the plan is several lots to several banks, which argues for slower. Outside view: about 30–35% named within 25 days. A *public* tender call before an award is less typical in Paraguayan practice, where processes surface at the award.

### Inside view

- The officials' own timeline (balance ~22 Nov, tender/transfer ~27 Nov) puts the event right at the cutoff. Official timelines in bank resolutions usually slip. Still, the pressure is strong: about USD 516 m of pension money (IPS) is frozen, Fitch put the sovereign on negative watch, and the Senate is under pressure. Recent statements by Schreiner and board member Acosta say the lots "se ofrecerán mediante una licitación". A public call between about 22 and 27 Nov is plausible, and so is a press-conference mention of invited banks. Either would count as "tender launched".
- As of 15 Nov (HOY), the transfer route was "todavía sin fechas ni entidades confirmadas". No pliego and no list of invited banks was found.
- Pension-fund assets could be placed directly with a named bank or public entity such as BNF. That is a route to "named" without a formal tender.

Net: no tender or name 0.42, tender only 0.30, named 0.28.

### Evidence

- 12 Nov: Schreiner reported about USD 600 m of sound assets. The plan is to tender and transfer lots to several local banks, avoiding a "megabanco", with the balance de exclusión in ~10 days [1][2]. Diario HOY put the transfer about 15 days out [3].
- 15 Nov: HOY reported the transfer mechanism still "under study", with no dates or entities [4]. EFE/Infobae, the same day: lots "se ofrecerán mediante una licitación", with no dates and no banks named [5].
- The bank list publicised for collecting payouts (Atlas, Basa, Itaú, Familiar, etc.) is excluded by the fine print [6].
- Cefisa precedent: 31 days from start to named recipients [7].
