## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-24

### What resolves YES

The question resolves to the number of UN member states shown as "In favour" in the First Committee recorded vote on the draft resolution "Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons" during the 81st session's action phase (27 Oct–3 Nov 2026). The source is the UN Meetings Coverage summary, or ICAN / Reaching Critical Will (RCW) reporting the official tally. Only the vote on the resolution as a whole counts: paragraph votes and the December plenary vote do not. If there is no recorded vote, the question is annulled. The scale runs from 130 to 155, and both ends are open, so a count of 129 or lower lands in the below-range bin.

**Note on the question background.** The background says 144 states voted in favour in 2024. That figure is wrong for this resolution. The UN press release of 1 Nov 2024 gives the TPNW draft (L.37) as **122–44–14** [2]. The 144–145 figure matches Japan's "common road map" resolution [2][7]. The real TPNW count has stayed at about 118–124 since 2020, so the most likely outcome sits *below* the question's 130 floor.

Since 2021 the mean is about 123 and the year-to-year standard deviation is about 1.5–2. The opposing bloc (the nuclear-armed states, NATO and other states under a nuclear umbrella, about 44) and the abstainers (about 12–16) have barely moved. Absences decide most of the year-to-year variation.

### Inside view

- **Upward pressure, small.** Tonga acceded in July 2026, making 75 states parties [6]. But Tonga, like most new parties, already voted yes. The first TPNW Review Conference (30 Nov–4 Dec 2026) may lead sponsors to work harder for votes. That could add one or two votes, at most.
- **Downward pressure, small.** The trend since 2022 is flat to slightly down: 124 → 122 → 121. The 2025 plenary vote was only 119 [1]. Absences from small delegations are unpredictable, and arrears-related loss of voting rights affects the plenary more than the Committee.
- **No sign of a bloc switching.** I found no 2026 report of any umbrella state or abstainer changing its position (search on 2026-10-24) [8].
- The question background's 2024 figure is a factual error, so this forecast does not anchor on the question's 130–155 scale.

I put the median at 122 and the IQR at 120–124. The tails run from 116 at the 5th percentile to 128.5 at the 95th. That leaves about 3–4% for ≥130, which covers a surprise switch by a group of abstainers or an error in the tally I relied on for 2025.

### What would change my mind

- A 2026 report that several European or umbrella states moved to abstain or vote yes, or that a large group of abstainers switched to yes. That would shift the distribution up, towards the high 120s.
- Evidence that the 2025 tally of 121 is wrong. A competing, unverified snippet mentions 124. Even so, this would move the median by only one or two votes.
