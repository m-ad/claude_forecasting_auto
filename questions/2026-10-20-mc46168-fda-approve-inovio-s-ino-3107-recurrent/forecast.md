## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-20

### What resolves YES

The question resolves YES if, by the end of 30 October 2026 (US Eastern), the FDA has granted marketing approval to INOVIO's INO-3107 for recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP) in adults. Accelerated approval counts, as does any other formulation or label that covers adult RRP. Evidence can be Drugs@FDA, an FDA announcement, an INOVIO press release or credible media. Everything else resolves NO: a Complete Response Letter (CRL, the FDA's formal non-approval), a PDUFA extension (the Prescription Drug User Fee Act goal date is 30 Oct), or simply no action by that date. As of 20 Oct 2026 no decision has been reported, so the question is mainly whether the FDA approves on or near the goal date.

### Outside view

For novel BLAs (Biologics License Applications) that reach their PDUFA date, first-cycle approval historically runs about 70–75%. For small-cap sponsors with single-arm data and no advisory committee it is lower, about 55–65%. The FDA meets 90–97% of its goal dates, so a delay or extension takes about 5% [7]. Conditional on a completed late-cycle meeting and label negotiations under way, approval is usually around 85%. That last figure is analyst lore, not a published statistic. My starting prior is about 60%.

### Inside view

Factors pushing the probability down:

- **Eligibility for accelerated approval is unresolved and structurally hard.** In its December 2025 filing letter the FDA said its preliminary view was that INOVIO had not justified accelerated-approval eligibility [1]. The cause is Precigen's Papzimeos, which received *full* approval in August 2025. Its single-arm complete-response rate was 51% (18/35) [5]. Accelerated approval then requires a meaningful advantage over available therapy.
  - INO-3107's year-1 rate of patients needing no surgery was 28% (50% in year 2). Its 72% figure counts 50–100% reductions in surgeries, a looser endpoint [3].
  - INOVIO's own 10-Q says it expects the FDA will *not* approve under the accelerated pathway unless it shows clinically meaningful benefit over Papzimeos [6].
  - INOVIO is not pursuing the traditional pathway [4].
- **The FDA has stayed silent on the key issue.** At the July 2026 informal meeting it did not discuss eligibility, and feedback on the confirmatory-trial design was still "forthcoming" as of August [2][3]. A confirmatory trial generally has to be agreed, and often under way, at the time of accelerated approval.
- **Label talks are unconfirmed.** INOVIO guided to label negotiations "in September", but nothing since 12 Aug confirms they began [4][6].
- **The market looks sceptical.** The stock was ~$0.99 on 16 Oct, down from $1.22 on 30 Sep, after a dilutive July raise at $0.95 [8]. That is weakly informative.

Factors pushing it up:

- The FDA completed the mid-cycle meeting (no new major issues), the late-cycle meeting and all pre-licensure inspections. The one inspection observation has reportedly been addressed [2][3].
- The FDA cancelled no review steps and planned no advisory committee.
- CBER (the FDA's Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research) has been more permissive since Vinay Prasad left in April 2026. Replimune's single-arm RP1 received accelerated approval in August 2026 [7].
- Draft guidance allows accelerated approval with similar efficacy via a different mechanism or a practical advantage. INOVIO argues that, unlike Papzimeos, INO-3107 needs no surgeries during the dosing window [3].

Net: I move down from about 60% to the mid-40s. A CRL citing accelerated-approval ineligibility is the main NO path (~45%), and a delay or extension adds ~6%. The uncertainty is large, because the eligibility outcome is essentially binary and hidden.

### Evidence

- 2025-12 (filing letter): FDA accepted the BLA under the accelerated approval program. Standard review, PDUFA 30 Oct 2026, no advisory committee planned, and the preliminary view that eligibility for accelerated approval was not justified [1].
- 2026-07-29 prospectus: "FDA may decide INO-3107 is not eligible…". INOVIO is not planning the traditional pathway. It raised $18.3M net at $0.95 [4].
- 2026-08-12 Q2 report and call: late-cycle meeting and all inspections complete. The FDA did not discuss eligibility at the July informal meeting. Confirmatory-trial feedback is pending. Label talks are anticipated in September. Cash lasts into late Q1 2027 [2][3].
- 2026-09-11: management still "expects" label negotiations to begin in September [9].
- 2026-10-06: the stock hub reports no company news since August [8].
- Papzimeos: full approval in August 2025, complete-response rate 51%, 7-year orphan exclusivity [5].
- Base rates: FDA meets about 90–97% of goal dates. The government is funded by a continuing resolution to about 11 Dec 2026 (scout report, single secondary source) [7].

### What would change my mind

- Confirmation that label negotiations actually started, or that the FDA agreed a confirmatory-trial design. Either would move me to about 70–80%.
