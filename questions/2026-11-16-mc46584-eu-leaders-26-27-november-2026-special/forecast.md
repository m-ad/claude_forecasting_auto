## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-16

### What resolves YES

The question resolves on the conclusions, the "Main results" page and any separate Ukraine text endorsed by heads of state or government at the 26–27 November 2026 Special European Council. Endorsement by all 27 or by a subset both count. It resolves YES only if those texts say leaders "agree" or "decide" that the EU will provide **new** 2027 financing for Ukraine beyond the €90bn Ukraine Support Loan, shown by at least one of: an amount, a named new instrument (for example a new 2027 EU loan), or an explicit commitment to make additional financing available. Opt-outs by some member states are allowed, and so is an agreement in principle whose legal act is left to the Council.

The following resolve NO:
- softer verbs: "call for", "look forward to", "invite further work", "will revert";
- a generic promise to "address" 2027 needs;
- endorsing the 30 Oct proposal to release the remaining €45bn of the existing loan (that is the €90bn loan's second half, not new money);
- postponement of the meeting.

The October 2026 conclusions used "look forward to … will revert", which would score NO. That is the wording to beat. The time left is one meeting, ten days from now. A second chance (17–18 Dec) falls outside the question.

### Outside view

The reference class is contested EU-level Ukraine financing items at the first European Council where they were tabled for decision:
- December 2023, the €50bn Ukraine Facility: blocked by Hungary, agreed in February 2024.
- October 2025, the reparations loan: deferred, then replaced by the €90bn loan in December 2025.
- The current case, tasked in October 2026 and deferred to this summit.

That is 0 of 3 agreed at the first attempt. Each item was eventually agreed, one summit later. A Laplace-smoothed rate of roughly 20–25% for "agreed at this specific meeting" is the starting point.

### Inside view

**Up (+12 pp):** This is a *special* summit with Ukraine's 2027 money as its headline item. Costa said on 23 Oct: "I assume that in November we will be able to make a decision" [3]. The Commission wants political agreement before year end [3]. A ready template exists: an EU-borrowing loan under enhanced cooperation, as in December 2025 [9]. Since a subset text counts, a Hungarian or Slovak veto alone cannot force NO.

**Down (−7 pp):**
- As of 9–16 Nov there is no formally tabled top-up proposal. Dombrovskis had only "options" and was "cautiously optimistic" [1, 2].
- Hungary (Magyar), Slovakia (Fico), Czechia (Babiš) and Bulgaria (Radev) all say they will not contribute [6, 7, 8].
- Merz and Dombrovskis want *all 27* to contribute, and Merz ties this to the 2028–34 budget (MFF) [4, 5]. That pushes toward a package deal with the MFF, which is unlikely to close in November. The 17–18 Dec European Council is already named as the fallback [3].
- Draft conclusions are likely to fall back on "address"/"revert" wording, which resolves NO.

**Net:** about 30%. The IQR of [22%, 38%] reflects uncertainty about whether leaders will sign an "agree in principle to a new loan of about €45bn" line with opt-outs while leaving the details to December.

### Evidence

- 15–16 Oct 2026 European Council: Commission tasked to cover the ~€70bn 2027 gap. Leaders "will revert" [10].
- 23 Oct: Costa expects a decision in November, with 17–18 Dec as the fallback, and EU coverage of about two-thirds (~€47bn) [3].
- 30 Oct: Commission proposes approving the remaining €45bn of the existing loan for 2027. This does not count [11].
- 9 Nov: Dombrovskis floats a ~€45bn top-up, a loan similar to the €90bn one, not yet presented to governments, and wants EU27 backing [1, 2].
- 9–11 Nov: Merz letter conditions the MFF on everyone contributing. Costa: "We will address this issue in the Special European Council on 26-27 November" [4, 5].
- 10 Nov onward: Magyar says Hungary cannot take on new debt, Fico calls the Merz link "blackmail", Babiš is negative, and Radev says Bulgaria cannot contribute [6, 7, 8].
- 13 Nov Dublin informal meeting: broad discussion only, no decision [12].

### What would change my mind

- **Toward YES:** draft conclusions leaked before 26 Nov containing "agrees to provide" or a figure, or a formal Commission top-up proposal tabled with a Coreper (EU ambassadors) green light. Either would push this above 50%.
- **Toward NO:** reports that the Ukraine item is being folded into the December MFF package, or that the draft says "will revert". Either would push this below 15%.
