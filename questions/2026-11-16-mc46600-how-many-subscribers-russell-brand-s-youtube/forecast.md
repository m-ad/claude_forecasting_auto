### Inside view

The trial pushed the rate up. The 10K notch from 6.62M to 6.61M fell on 2 Nov, and the next fell on 11 Nov. That is about 1.1K/day across the verdict (5 Nov [3]) and the sentencing (9 Nov [4]), against roughly one notch a month before. Daily views jumped from 0.1–0.3M to 0.9–4.5M on 6–13 Nov, then eased to 0.7–1.0M by 14–16 Nov [1]. Shocks like this usually decay, and the channel has no new uploads to replace lost subscribers. If 11–16 Nov ran at about −800/day and the rest of the window runs at 350–700/day, the count on 27 Nov is about 6,597K–6,601K. That sits right on the 6.60M/6.59M boundary. Meta disabled his Instagram after the conviction [5]. YouTube's precedent, the Austin Jones case [6], is to terminate a channel when the conviction is closely tied to its content. That isn't clearly the case here, so I put termination in the window at about 5%. Termination would freeze the reading at 6.6M. Combined: P(6.60M) ≈ 50%, P(6.59M) ≈ 40%, P(≤6.58M) ≈ 5%, P(≥6.61M) ≈ 5%. The upside comes from Musk/Megyn Kelly-driven support [7] or a new upload.
