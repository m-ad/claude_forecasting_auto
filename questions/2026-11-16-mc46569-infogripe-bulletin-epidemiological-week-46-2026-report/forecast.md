## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-16

### What resolves YES

YES if the national-level text (highlights list and/or "Situação nacional") of the Resumo do Boletim InfoGripe for SE 46 2026 (15–21 Nov 2026, expected Thu 26 Nov) says Brazil-wide SRAG confirmed for COVID-19 shows growth ("sinal de crescimento"/"crescimento") on the 6-week or the 3-week trend. Stabilisation, oscillation, fall, deceleration ("desaceleração do crescimento"), interruption, or silence → NO; state-only, age-group-only or deaths-only growth → NO. Not published by 27 Nov 23:59 UTC → annulled. Period arithmetic: the latest published edition is SE 44 (1–7 Nov, data to 07/11, released 12 Nov); SE 45 comes out 19 Nov; the target SE 46 is **two editions** after the last one seen, with data to 21 Nov. The SE 44 text reads, verbatim: "há um sinal de crescimento dos casos confirmados por metapneumovírus e por Covid-19". Had the question been about SE 44, it would resolve YES.

### Outside view

Reference class: InfoGripe national COVID-19 SRAG growth signals that have been running for two or more editions, and whether they are still there two editions later. Past waves: the 2023 late-year wave grew until about SE 44, and the SE 46 2023 bulletin already reported a pause in adults and deceleration in the South and Bahia. In Nov 2024 nine UFs were growing at SE 45, but by late November most of the Centro-Sul was falling. A national growth phase usually lasts about 4–8 editions. Given a signal that started around SE 42–43, the chance it is still there at SE 46 is about 55–65%. Unconditionally, a national Covid growth signal shows up in maybe 20–30% of weeks. n is small and the history comes from news summaries: a rough prior.

### Inside view

For persistence: the wave is still spreading. SE 43 named 5 UFs; SE 44 names DF, MG, SP, RJ and PR plus Bahia (more and larger states). The SARS-CoV-2 share of positives rose from 8.5% (SE 43) to 11.5% (SE 44). São Paulo's rise only began in the second half of October, and private hospitals reported more demand in early November. The 6-week trend for SE 46 (weeks 41–46) will still contain the whole rise unless counts fall sharply in SE 45–46. Either horizon is enough for YES. Against: Mato Grosso, the first state to rise, is already decelerating. Counts are small, so the nowcast can flip to "estabilização". The 2023 wave turned to deceleration around SE 46. The bulletin could also call it "desaceleração" while counts still rise, and that resolves NO. I also allow ~5% for the highlights covering other viruses and saying nothing national about Covid. Net: 58%.

### Evidence

- SE 44 bulletin (data to 07/11/2026): national growth signal in SRAG from metapneumovirus and Covid-19, at low levels. Growth in DF, MG, SP, RJ, PR and BA; MT decelerating; SARS-CoV-2 11.5% of positives in the last 4 weeks, 4.0% year to date [1].
- SE 43 coverage (5–10 Nov 2026): Covid SRAG rising in MT, DF, SP, RJ, SC; 8.5% of positives [2][3].
- São Paulo: the rise started in the second half of October. Hospital Santa Catarina-Paulista had 98 positives in October, 57 in September and 18 in August [4].
- Ministry of Health SE 41–43: a slight rise in Covid flu-like illness, with rising trends in 18 states plus DF [6].
- 2023 analogue: the SE 46 bulletin reported a pause and deceleration after growth up to SE 44 [5].

### What would change my mind

The SE 45 edition (19 Nov) is not available before close. If it had said "desaceleração" or "estabilização" for national Covid, I would go to ~25%; if it showed growth extending to more states, ~75%. A sharp fall in São Paulo state's SRAG panel in mid-November would also push this down.
