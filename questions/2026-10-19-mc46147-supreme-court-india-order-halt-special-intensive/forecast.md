## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-19

### What resolves YES

YES only if, by 23:59 IST on 29 October 2026, the Supreme Court of India has issued an interim or final order that is still in force and that stays, halts, suspends or discontinues the Election Commission of India's (ECI) Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls. The order can cover the whole exercise or its core step of deleting names or finalising the rolls. It can apply nationwide or to a single state or union territory (UT). Procedural directions do not count. That covers disclosure of the lists of voters sent notices, extra filters before notices, re-inclusion mechanisms, notice to the ECI, adjournments and reserved orders. A pause limited to issuing notices also does not count. Resolution rests on credible reporting and the Court's own record.

### Outside view

Indian courts almost never stay an ongoing exercise by a constitutional body such as the ECI over its electoral rolls. The Supreme Court's own record on the SIR since mid-2025 shows the same pattern. It refused interim stays of the Bihar SIR in July 2025 and of the Kerala SIR in November 2025. In February 2026 it ordered the West Bengal SIR to "continue without any hindrance". On 27 May 2026 it upheld the SIR on the merits in *ADR v ECI*. Its usual pattern is to criticise the ECI and order procedural fixes, such as extended deadlines, accepting Aadhaar, disclosure, or extra safeguards, while letting the exercise continue. That gives about 0 stays in 5 or more chances to stay it, which points to a base rate of a few percent per 10-day window, with an open hearing scheduled.

### Inside view

- The one hearing scheduled inside the window is on **28 October**, on the Bhardwaj/Johri petition about Delhi [3][4]. That plea seeks **disclosure** of the electors sent notices, with reasons, and a re-inclusion route that does not require Form 6. It does not seek a halt [4]. The Bench said it is "thinking of passing an order that will take care of it". The most likely meaning is a disclosure or procedure order, which the fine print says resolves NO.
- The Rahul Gandhi/INDIA bloc petition does ask for suspension across states. However, on 15 October the Court only issued notice and tagged it with the pending matters. It also flagged one petitioner's conduct and stressed that issuing notice "does not mean condemning" anything [1][2]. I found no reported next date inside the window. The ECI's reply would normally take weeks.
- Timing removes the main reason to stay the process. Delhi's claims and objections run until 30 October, and the final roll is due on 4 December [4]. The Court can wait to see the ECI's reply before any finalisation, so it has no need to halt anything by 29 October.
- Upward risks: the CJI's sharp language about "mechanical" notices, the street protests, and an order on 28 October that could be broad. An order barring final publication of the Delhi roll until disclosure, or barring deletions of people sent notices until a reasoned order, might count as halting a "core deletion/finalisation step". The question was written by an LLM, so it may read such an order loosely. That is the main tail. I put about 3% on it.

Net: **3%**.

### Evidence

- 15 Oct 2026: the Supreme Court (CJI Surya Kant and Justice Bagchi) issued notice to the ECI, the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and both Election Commissioners on the Rahul Gandhi/INDIA bloc writ, W.P.(C) 1351/2026. The writ seeks suspension pending adjudication. The Court tagged it with the related pleas, including those of Owaisi and Digvijaya Singh. It did not grant an interim stay [1][2].
- 14 Oct 2026: the Court moved the Delhi SIR notices plea up from 30 to 28 October. The relief sought is disclosure and re-inclusion, not a stay. Delhi's claims and objections were extended to 30 October, with the final roll on 4 December [4].
- 22 Sep 2026: the Court said Delhi notices looked "almost mechanically" issued and asked the ECI to consider extra safeguards. It issued notice and listed the matter for two weeks later. There was no stay [3].
- Earlier record: no stay of the Bihar SIR (July 2025), the Kerala SIR (November 2025) or the West Bengal SIR (February 2026) [5][6].
- My searches found no report of any stay order up to 19 October 2026.
