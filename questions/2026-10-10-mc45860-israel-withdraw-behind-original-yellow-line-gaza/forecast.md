### What resolves YES
Source: Metaculus criteria and fine print, read through the anonymous `/api-proxy/` post JSON on 2026-10-10 (`raw/metaculus-post-2026-10-10.json`) [19]. **YES** requires both of the following, reported by credible sources before 2027-01-01. (1) Israeli forces have **announced** a withdrawal to a position **behind** the Yellow Line, meaning the specific line of the October 2025 ceasefire map as illustrated by OCHA. (2) They have **physically withdrawn** to such a position, so they no longer hold military control of the vacated area. Fine print: any distance behind the line qualifies if it is announced and maintained for at least 24 hours, and **"the status of the 'Orange Lines' is irrelevant."** The background states the intent: Israeli-held land between the Yellow Line and the Green Line must fall **below 53%** of Gaza. So a return from today's advanced positions (about 65–70%) *to* the original line does **not** resolve YES. Israel must cede ground it held on 2025-10-10, which is the plan's next withdrawal stage, conditioned on Hamas decommissioning. Edge cases: (a) a *local* announced pullback behind the line in one sector, while positions elsewhere stay advanced, could satisfy "any distance behind" literally but not the <53% framing. I give it partial credit. (b) An unannounced de facto pullback fails the "announced" requirement. (c) The OCHA map of the original line is the yardstick, not whatever line the IDF currently marks with blocks.

### Outside view
Reference class: Israeli withdrawals from territory held in an active conflict zone before a counterpart's demanded precondition is met, within ~3 months, absent a signed agreement being implemented. Related cases: Gaza 2005 (took years of preparation), Lebanon 2000 (announced months earlier), Israeli pullbacks after the 2025 Gaza ceasefire (executed within 24h of cabinet approval, Oct 2025), Lebanon post-Nov-2024 ceasefire (60-day deadline missed; troops kept 5 hilltop positions into 2026 — from memory, not verified this session). Since the Oct 2025 ceasefire, the line has moved *outward* only (53% → 60% → 65–70%). Base rate for a retreat *behind* a line that the army calls a "new border" (Zamir, Dec 2025), i.e. ceding ground held since the ceasefire's first day, within 80 days: low single digits. Status-quo prior ≈ 2%.

### Inside view
YES needs a chain. (1) Hamas begins verifiable decommissioning, or Israel waives the disarm-first condition under US pressure: about 15% by mid-December. The roadmap was agreed on 31 Jul and has stalled, the international stabilization force (ISF) is unassembled, and the technocratic committee (NCAG) is barred from Gaza. (2) Given that, Israel's withdrawal goes *past* the original line rather than just back to it: about 12%. Under the roadmap the first stages would plausibly vacate post-ceasefire gains, which doesn't count, and deeper stages are tied to heavy-weapons and tunnel decommissioning that a Board of Peace official put at 200–350 days. (3) It is announced, executed and held for 24h before Jan 1: about 60%. Product ≈ 1.1%. Plus three side paths. The 27 Oct election yields a government willing to pull back: the coalition takes weeks and nothing moves before then, about +0.3pp. A local announced pullback behind the line counts under a literal reading: the Rafah ISF pilot is confined "strictly outside the Yellow Line" [20], so there's no live candidate, about +0.5pp. Resolver error or other: about +0.2pp. Total ≈ 2%.
