## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-02

### What resolves YES

YES if Pete Hegseth still holds the Senate-confirmed office of Secretary of Defense (styled "Secretary of War") at **18:00 ET / 23:00 UTC on Thursday 12 November 2026**. The source is the defense.gov leadership page, with credible reporting as the fallback. NO if he has resigned, been dismissed, or otherwise left the office by then. An announcement that he will leave at a later date does **not** count, and a temporary delegation of authority to the Deputy Secretary (Feinberg) does not count either. The window runs from today (2 Nov) and covers 10.8 days. It includes the 3 November midterms and the first four days of the lame-duck session (Congress returns 9 Nov). Impeachment cannot remove him in time: there would have to be a House vote, which Speaker Johnson has said will be tabled, and then a Senate trial. The Blackburn Senate-seat route comes too late, because Tennessee governors take office in January. So NO needs Trump to fire him, or Hegseth to resign, **with effect by 12 Nov**. The defense.gov page returned HTTP 403 today. As of 2 Nov no credible source reports that he has left.

### Outside view

Reference class: a sitting cabinet secretary staying in office over a 10-day window. Ordinary cabinet turnover is about 10–15 % a year, which gives roughly 99.5 % survival over 10 days. That class understates the risk here, because this window opens right after a midterm. Post-midterm exits under Trump have been fast and effective immediately: Attorney General Sessions was out on 7 Nov 2018, the day after the midterms, with an acting AG installed the same day [5]. Defense Secretary Esper was fired by tweet on 9 Nov 2020, effective immediately (from memory, not re-fetched). The Bush-era example ran the other way: Rumsfeld's resignation was announced on 8 Nov 2006, but he stayed in office until 18 Dec [6]. For an embattled secretary at a post-election decision point, a better prior is roughly 90–95 % survival over 10 days.

### Inside view

- **Pressure on him is real and comes from both parties.** Massie's impeachment push resumes the week of 9 Nov [3]. Puck reported on 25 Oct that some GOP senators hope Trump removes him first [4]. Sen. Tillis wants him fired, and Senate Democrats reportedly want his ouster as the price of lame-duck defense deals [7]. Polymarket puts an exit **announced** by 31 Dec at 31 % [1]. On 23 Sep, Kalshi had a departure announced before 1 Dec at 24 % [2]. GJOpen (Good Judgment Open) has him ceasing to be Secretary before 1 Dec at 31 %, a figure that has fallen 20 points in a month [8].
- **Converting those prices to this window.** Assume about 25–30 % for an announcement by year-end, with perhaps 35–40 % of it falling in the decisive post-election week. That gives about 10–12 % for an announcement by 12 Nov. A Trump firing tends to take effect immediately, as with Sessions and Esper; a negotiated resignation or Senate-seat exit would come with a later date. I estimate 55–60 % of announcements would be effective by 12 Nov, so P(NO) is about 6–7 %. I add about 1 pp for resolution noise, such as a stale defense.gov page or an ambiguous "acting" arrangement.
- **Arguments for him staying.** Trump defended him on 29 Oct, according to the question text, and earlier called him "one of my best" (Aug 2026). The Pentagon dismissed the exit talk as "beltway gossip" [4]. No successor is being vetted, and the earlier heir-apparent, Army Secretary Driscoll, resigned in August. Hegseth's own preferred exit, the Tennessee Senate seat, only opens in January, which gives him reason to stay until then. The "fired the day after the election" prediction comes from a Democratic commentator, Max Rose [4].
- **Net:** P(YES) ≈ 92 %.

### What would change my mind

- **Bad GOP midterm night (3–4 Nov).** If the GOP does badly and Trump makes a Sessions- or Esper-style immediate move, the forecast goes to NO. This is the main risk.
- **Reports of a successor being vetted, or an acting-Secretary plan,** in the days after the election would cut P(YES) sharply.
- **Silence through 5–6 Nov** would make YES very likely, because the remaining days are lame-duck procedure and impeachment cannot finish in time.
