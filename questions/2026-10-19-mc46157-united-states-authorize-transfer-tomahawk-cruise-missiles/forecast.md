## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-19

### What resolves YES

YES only if, between 17 October 2026 and 23:59 UTC on 30 October 2026, the President, the White House or the Pentagon makes a publicly reported, actual decision to provide Tomahawk cruise missiles to Ukraine, whether from US stocks, by sale, or through a third country or NATO mechanism. Examples are an order to deliver or a signed or announced sale. "Considering", "not ruling out", a Pentagon-only green light, or a general resumption of arms sales to allies do not count. Delivery is not required. If credible sources report no such authorization by the deadline, the question resolves NO. The window is 11 days.

### Outside view

The US has never authorized Tomahawks for Ukraine. The request dates from September 2025, so it has stood unauthorized for about 13 months, through the period of strongest pressure: October 2025, when the Pentagon cleared a transfer and Trump still declined [1][2]. If the per-month hazard over that period is around 3% or less, an 11-day window gives about 1%. Over the same period the US approved lesser long-range systems (ERAM, 29 August 2025 [7]) but kept refusing Tomahawks specifically. Base rate: about 1%.

### Inside view

The current evidence points further toward NO:
- **Stockpiles.** The US fired more than 1,000 Tomahawks in the 2026 Iran war, and CSIS puts the return to pre-war inventory at about four years [5]. In September 2026 the US warned allies of delivery delays of up to five years; Germany's own Tomahawk order is affected [6]. Trump said on 15–16 October that allied weapons purchases are paused until US stocks are "really" replenished (per the question background).
- **Politics.** US–Ukraine relations are at a low point. Trump said on Newsmax on 15 October that he is "not a big fan" of Zelensky and called Ukraine's refinery strikes "a disgrace" [3][4]. Envoys reportedly threatened to cut intelligence sharing and block arms supplied through PURL, the European-funded scheme for buying US weapons for Ukraine [8]. Trump is also brokering an "energy truce" and a Russian diesel deal with Putin [9]. Authorizing a deep-strike weapon now would contradict the whole current direction of his policy.
- **What could still produce YES.** Trump uses the Tomahawk threat as leverage against Putin, as he did in 2025. A sudden breakdown with Moscow could prompt an announcement, but even in October 2025 he threatened and did not authorize. A loosely worded threat could also be misread as authorization by the LLM resolver, but the criteria explicitly exclude "considering" language.

I hold it at about 1.5%. The extra half-point over the base rate covers Trump's volatility and the risk that the resolver reads a statement too loosely.

### Evidence

- 31 Oct 2025: CNN reported that the Pentagon gave the White House a green light and left the decision to Trump. Trump said no, "at least not yet" [1][2].
- 29 Aug 2025: the State Department approved a sale of 3,350 ERAM missiles to Ukraine, so a long-range missile was approved where Tomahawks were not [7].
- Feb–Apr 2026: more than 1,000 Tomahawks were fired in the Iran war, with about four years needed to backfill [5].
- 19 Sep 2026: Bloomberg, via Ukrainska Pravda, reported that the US warned allies of delays of up to five years and that about a third of its Tomahawks were used against Iran [6].
- 9–14 Oct 2026: envoys reportedly threatened to cut intelligence sharing and block PURL deliveries over the refinery strikes; the US denied making the threat [8].
- 11 Oct 2026: Trump announced an energy truce, after a 9 October diesel deal with Putin [9].
- 15–16 Oct 2026: on Newsmax, Trump said he is "not a big fan" of Zelensky [3][4].
- I searched for October 2026 news of any Tomahawk authorization, myself and through two scouts, and found none.

### What would change my mind

- A collapse of the Putin track: Russia rejects the energy truce outright and Trump publicly turns on Moscow, together with renewed Tomahawk threats.
- A report of an allied purchase (for example German or Dutch) of Tomahawks for Ukraine through PURL, signed off by Washington.
