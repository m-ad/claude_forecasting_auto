## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-02

### What resolves YES

The question resolves on the Dáil Official Report for Thursday 12 November 2026, a single sitting; anything after midnight counts as part of it. There are four mutually exclusive outcomes:

1. **Granted**: "That the Bill be now read a Second Time" is put and carried on the night.
2. **Refused**: the motion is put and declared lost, or a timed or reasoned amendment deferring or declining the Second Reading is carried on the night.
3. **Division claimed and postponed**: a division on the motion or an amendment is claimed and deferred under SO 85(2) to the next week's voting block.
4. **No question put**: the debate is adjourned, the item is not taken, or the Bill is withdrawn.

Two arithmetic consequences follow. A contested Thursday private members' business (PMB) vote almost never resolves "Refused" on the night, because divisions are deferred. So "Refused" needs the question to be declared lost by voice with no division claimed, which is very rare. Any opposition by any TD that leads to a claimed division resolves as option 3 whatever the later vote. The question text says the item was listed for 12 Nov in the Business Committee reports of 2, 6 and 19 Oct [1].

### Outside view

I have no clean dataset of 34th-Dáil Thursday PMB outcomes; oireachtas.ie returned 403 to every fetch. My working reference class is Second Stage debates on PMBs that the Government does not oppose, using the recent 2026 cases I found:

- Sinn Féin birth-certificate bill, April 2026 [6]
- Aontú presidential-voting bill, April 2026 [6]
- SF historic-convictions bill, 16 Apr 2026, not opposed by Justice Minister O'Callaghan [6]

These usually end "Question put and agreed to" on the night. Opposed PMBs end with a division claimed and postponed, as with the Children's Health Bill on 15 Oct 2026 [1]. For a generic Thursday PMB I'd put roughly 50% granted, 35% division postponed, and 15% not put or adjourned. This is a memory-based prior, flagged as such.

### Inside view

- **Government-party sponsor.** Barry Ward is a Fine Gael (FG) TD, and FG is in government. The Programme for Government commits to "stronger laws to combat the spiking of drinks" [3]. No Government spiking Bill exists [1]. The Government did not oppose this same Bill at Seanad Second Stage in May 2023 [5], and it passed every Seanad stage without amendment [1]. Openly opposing a coalition partner's flagship criminal-law PMB weeks after FG publicly pressed for it [2] would be unusual. A timed amendment is possible, since the Minister says existing poisoning law covers spiking [3], but I put it at only a few percent. This moves "granted" up to about 73%.
- **Division risk.** Sinn Féin has a rival Bill with a 10-year maximum penalty [4]. SF and others will likely support a standalone offence while criticising the details; voting against creating a spiking offence would be bad politics. A single TD could still claim a division, so about 11%.
- **Not put.** The item could be swapped out, the sitting disrupted, or the debate adjourned without the question being put. The March 2026 Order of Business set no explicit conclusion time for the Thursday evening PMB [7]. About 13%.
- **Refused on the night** needs a voice vote lost or an amendment carried with no division claimed: about 3%.
