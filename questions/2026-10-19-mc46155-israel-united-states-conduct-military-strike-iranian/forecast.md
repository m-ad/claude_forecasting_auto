## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-19

### What resolves YES

The question resolves YES if, between 00:00 UTC on 29 October and 23:59 UTC on 30 October 2026, Israeli or US armed forces deliver at least one munition (air, missile, drone or other strike) that lands on Iran's internationally recognised land territory. Credible reporting or an official US or Israeli statement must attribute it. Unclaimed strikes count if credible reporting attributes them, and so do errant impacts. None of the following count: strikes at sea or on vessels (including blockade enforcement against ships), interceptions, cyber operations, sabotage without a munition, strikes by proxies, and anything outside the 48-hour window. I read the criteria literally: one small CENTCOM "self-defense" strike on a coastal launcher would be enough.

### Outside view

Since the 8 April ceasefire (about 195 days), US strikes on Iranian land came in clusters: about 7 May, about 25 May, 8–11 June, 28 June, a nightly campaign from about 8 to 29 July, and 30 August – 1 September. That is roughly 25–35 strike-days, or about 15 % of days. A naive two-day window would give roughly 25 % [7][8]. That figure overstates the risk, because the strikes cluster heavily and the current period is a lull. No US land strike has been confirmed since about 1–3 September, which is about 7 weeks [7][9]. Israel and Iran have not exchanged fire since June [4]. Earlier lulls broke after 1–4 weeks, usually after Iranian attacks on ships or US troops. Within a quiet period, a reasonable base rate for a specific 48-hour window is about 5–10 %.

### Inside view

Adjustments from the lull base rate:

- **Down: an explicit US pledge covering the window.** On 8 October Trump posted that the US "will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections… November 3rd" [1][2]. His aides reportedly see "November" as the priority for any escalation, and a third carrier arrives only around the end of October [3][9]. Trump has broken stated intentions before, including on 28 February, so the pledge is not binding. Still, a deliberate large operation on 29–30 October, five days before the vote, would contradict his own public commitment.
- **Down: Israel's posture.** Netanyahu called a pre-election strike "not convenient for us, it's no good" [4]. Israeli officials say Israel cannot strike without Trump's approval [5]. Right after the 27 October vote Israel will be forming a coalition. A solo Israeli strike on 29–30 October would cut across the US pledge.
- **Up: tail risks.** First, the IRGC keeps attacking Hormuz shipping (UKMTO recorded about 20 attacks since 1 October [10]). A strike on US forces could bring a limited "self-defense" strike on a coastal launcher, as on 30 August, and that alone resolves YES. So far, though, the October attacks on shipping have drawn only blockade enforcement at sea. Second, an Iranian missile barrage on Israel around its election day [6] would almost certainly bring Israeli retaliation within days. Third, there are reports of joint planning (Axios, about 13 October, denied by the White House) [5].

Rough decomposition for the window: US "self-defense" strike about 3 %; deliberate early start of the post-election campaign about 2 %; Israeli strike, solo or after an Iranian attack, about 1.5 %. Total about 6 %.

### Evidence

- 8 Oct 2026: Trump says no attack on Iran before the 3 November midterms, citing "productive discussions"; the blockade continues [1][2].
- About 8 Oct: Axios reports CENTCOM was told to prepare for possible major operations with no date set; one source said the chances before the elections are "not high" [3].
- 14 Oct: Netanyahu rejects a pre-election preemptive strike; Reuters says Israel and Iran have not exchanged fire since June [4].
- 18 Oct: Trump says the US could "soon finish the job"; CENTCOM has redirected 138 vessels as of 17 October; Iran says its 7-day Hormuz plan stands and it is reviewing US comments [10]. No strikes reported.
- 25 Sep (GlobalSecurity Day 210, snippet): 18 consecutive operational periods without a US or coalition strike on Iranian land [9].
- Last confirmed US land strikes: 30 Aug (Larak Island launchers) and 1 Sep (IRGC sites across Iran) [7][8].

### What would change my mind

- An IRGC attack that kills or injures US personnel, or hits a US warship, in the days before 29 October. That would make a limited US "self-defense" strike on coastal sites likely, regardless of the midterm pledge.
- An Iranian missile attack on Israel around the 27 October election, which would make Israeli retaliation inside the window plausible (roughly 30–50 % conditional).
- Talks collapsing together with credible reports that Trump is moving the start of operations earlier.
