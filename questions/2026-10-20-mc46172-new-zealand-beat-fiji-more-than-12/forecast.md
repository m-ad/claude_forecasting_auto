### Outside view

There is no clean reference class. The direct head-to-head record is tiny: Fiji won 4–2 in the 2017 World Cup quarter-final, and New Zealand won 24–18 in the 2022 quarter-final after trailing 18–6. Neither game cleared 12 points, and both were high-stakes knockouts [3][4]. (The question text calls these the only two previous meetings. A "2013 semi-final 40–4" result I had from memory could not be verified: in 2013 Fiji played Australia in the semi-final, not New Zealand.) A broader class is the Kiwis against second-tier Pacific sides. In the 2025 Pacific Championships the Kiwis beat Samoa by 6 and 22 and Tonga by 26, so 2 of 3 cleared the line [5]. Australia have beaten Fiji by 32 or more points in every World Cup meeting since 2008 [6]. Taken together, my prior is that a strong favourite covers a 12.5-point line roughly 55–65% of the time.

### Inside view

- **New Zealand are close to Australia's level.** They lost the opener 22–24 after dominating long stretches. They have lost Kalyn Ponga (broken arm, out for the tournament) but still have a deep NRL squad [1][2].
- **Fiji are good but weakened.** They beat the Cook Islands 40–12 (28–0 at half-time) with an NRL spine of Koroisau, Wakeham, Saifiti and Turuva [7][8]. They are missing Viliame Kikau, Tui Kamikamica, Jahream Bula and the three ineligible Fainu brothers [8][9]. Bookmakers price Fiji as large outsiders: 500/1 to win Pool A against 50/1 for New Zealand, and a tipster line of Australia −44.5 against Fiji [6][10].
- **The stakes are high.** If, as expected, Fiji lose to Australia and New Zealand beat the Cook Islands, this round-3 game becomes a straight playoff for the second semi-final spot. Fiji will be fully motivated, and that setting is where they kept the 2017 and 2022 games tight. This pulls the probability down from what a pure ratings gap implies.
- **Model.** I found no match line, so I estimated the expected New Zealand margin at about 19 points (standard deviation 4 on that estimate) with a match-level standard deviation of 15 points, typical for rugby league. That gives P(margin ≥ 13) ≈ 0.66. At an expected margin of 14 it is 0.54; at 23 it is 0.76. I shade the result down to 0.62 for the knockout-like stakes and New Zealand's history of tight games against Fiji.

- 2026-10-18: Fiji 40–12 Cook Islands in Newcastle; 7 tries to 2, 28–0 at half-time [7].
- 2026-10-20: New Zealand made 6 changes for the Cook Islands game on 25 October, with Keano Kini at fullback [11].
- Fiji squad: Kikau (ankle), Kamikamica (illness) and Bula (shoulder) are out, and the Fainu brothers are ineligible [8][9].
- Fiji play Australia in Brisbane on 25 October, six days before the New Zealand game [6].
- Head to head at World Cups: 2–4 (2017) and 24–18 (2022), both quarter-finals [3][4].
- Alphr's tournament model gives New Zealand a 95% chance of reaching the semi-finals. That implies a New Zealand win probability over Fiji of roughly 90% or more, which in turn suggests an expected margin of about 20 points [12].
- Pre-tournament outright odds: New Zealand +210 to 7/2, Fiji 500/1 to +50000. Fiji were about 16-point favourites against the Cook Islands and won by 28 [7][10].

### What would change my mind

- **Bookmaker handicap.** A line of NZ −24 or more would push me towards 0.72; a line of −12 or less towards 0.48.
- **Australia v Fiji result (25 October).** A competitive Fiji showing, or New Zealand injuries against the Cook Islands, would lower the forecast. A blowout loss by Fiji, or Fiji resting players because they are already eliminated on head-to-head, would raise it.
