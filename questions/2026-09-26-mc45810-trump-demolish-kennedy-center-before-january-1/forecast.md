## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-09-26

### What resolves YES
YES if the Kennedy Center is "substantially demolished" before 2027-01-01. Per the fine print,
"substantially demolished" means more than a *de minimis* (trivially small) portion of the
**structure** is intentionally destroyed. The question author (darkives, comment 2026-09-26)
clarified that "structure" means the building itself: **gutting interiors (seats, finishes,
mechanical systems) does not count**. So a YES needs walls, roof, facade or structural frame
intentionally taken down. The bar is not the whole building: an East-Wing-style start of
demolition that brings down a real part of the shell would probably be enough. No official data
source; resolution rests on news reports. Edge cases: (a) removing corroded exterior soffits or
facade panels during "emergency repairs" (probably de minimis/repair, but arguable);
(b) a partial teardown started in late December (would count if more than de minimis).
The title says "Trump", but the operative criterion is only that the building is demolished.

### Outside view
There is no clean reference class. Three partial ones:
1. **Explicit demolition threats by US presidents against federal landmarks within ~3 months.**
   n≈0 historically. Not usable.
2. **Trump's second-term threat-to-action conversion on built-environment projects.** The East
   Wing: in July 2025 Trump said the ballroom "won't interfere" with the existing building; it was
   razed starting 2025-10-20, within days and without prior review [11][12]. That is a precedent
   for surprise speed. But the East Wing had no court order, and the White House is exempt from
   National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA) Section 106 review under Section 107. The Kennedy
   Center has a court order in place and no such exemption.
3. **Large federal building demolitions under active litigation plus a preliminary injunction.**
   Demolition within 3 months, before the merits are resolved, is rare: base rate well under 5%.

Status-quo prior for a 96-day window, with an injunction plus a 30-day notice order in force:
**~3–5%**.

### Inside view
YES needs one of these paths before 2026-12-31:

- **A. Lawful path (notice → courts don't stop it → demolition starts).**
  P(administration files a demolition notice by ~2026-11-30, which leaves ≥30 days) ≈ 12%.
  × P(Judge Cooper, the D.C. Circuit and the Supreme Court all decline to block it, or a Supreme
  Court emergency stay lets it go ahead) ≈ 20%. Plaintiffs include the DC Preservation League
  and 8 architecture/preservation groups with NHPA and statutory claims; Cooper has ruled against
  the board repeatedly. × P(wrecking actually begins on more than a de minimis part before
  Jan 1 | allowed) ≈ 60%. → **≈1.4%**.
- **B. Fait accompli (defying or evading the notice order).** The administration has
  formally complied with Cooper's orders so far: it filed the sworn status report and delayed
  renovation. Open contempt over a JFK memorial, with Republican appropriators opposed, is costly.
  → **≈0.7%**.
- **C. Resolution edge: "emergency repairs" or renovation remove more than de minimis structure**
  (soffits, facade elements) and the resolver counts it. → **≈0.8%**.
- **D. Genuine structural emergency** (engineer declares imminent collapse and part is taken
  down). Walter P. Moore's report as described cites hazards, not collapse risk. → **≈0.5%**.

Sum ≈ **3.4%**.

Actor incentives: Trump's stated goal is **his name on the building**. Demolition is a lever to
get it ("if we don't do that … It'll end up being ripped down" [2]). His own 2026-09-15 Truth
Social post puts the next step after the D.C. Circuit rules and, if that ruling goes against him,
after the Supreme Court [9]. On that timeline the threat matters after a final loss, and a
final loss is unlikely before 2027. The midterm elections are 2026-11-03, and razing a JFK memorial
beforehand is electorally costly. That leaves only the ~8-week lame-duck period, and the 30-day
notice eats most of it.
