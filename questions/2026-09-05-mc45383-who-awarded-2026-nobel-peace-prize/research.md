# Research log — Who will be awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize?

## Question mechanics

- **Type**: multiple choice, 11 options: Doctors Without Borders · Donald J. Trump · ICC / ICJ ·
  Pope Leo XIV · Save the Children · Sudan Emergency Response Rooms · Mykola Kuleba / Save Ukraine ·
  UNRWA · Yulia Navalnaya · **Other organization** · **Other individual**.
- **Announcement**: Friday **9 October 2026, 11:00 CEST**, Norwegian Nobel Institute, Oslo.
  Ceremony 10 December 2026. Prize 9.0m SEK.
- **Shared prize rule (fine print, load-bearing)**: resolves as the option naming *one of* the
  recipients. **If more than one recipient is named in the options, or none is, it resolves
  according to the recipient listed first in the official announcement.** 34 of 106 prizes have
  been shared, so this is roughly a 1-in-3 contingency, not an edge case.
- **Annulled** if no prize is awarded.
- **Edge cases**:
  - "Mykola Kuleba / Save Ukraine" names an individual **and** his organisation in one option —
    either winning resolves it. PRIO's 2026 recommendation is Kuleba **and Save the Children
    jointly**, so if the Committee does exactly that, resolution turns on announcement order.
  - "ICC / ICJ" bundles two distinct institutions; PRIO also recommends them jointly.
  - Trump's option is the only one where a *shared* award (e.g. with a co-signatory of a peace
    framework) is plausible, again turning on ordering.
- Nominations: **287 candidates — 208 individuals, 79 organisations.** Nominations are secret for
  50 years; nominators (parliamentarians, ministers, ICJ members, professors, past laureates) may
  self-publicise, which is why the visible "shortlists" are a biased sample of a secret pool.

**P(organisation wins under the first-listed rule) ≈ 30%** is the base rate. Any forecast pushing
much past ~45% needs a specific reason.

≈ **4–5 of 10**. The pre-announcement *favourite* has won perhaps **1–2 times in 10**. This is the
single most important calibration fact for the question: **the two "Other" buckets should carry
roughly half the probability mass**, and any market board concentrating 50%+ on three named
organisations is over-confident.

**The PRIO Director's Shortlist is the honourable exception.** Same-year hits since 2014: Malala
(2014), Mukwege + Murad (2018), Abiy (2019), Memorial (2022), Mohammadi (2023), Nihon Hidankyo
(2024) → **6 of 11 years ≈ 55%** for a five-item list. (Wikipedia's table also marks later-year
hits — Santos listed 2015 won 2016, WFP listed 2018 won 2020, Muratov listed 2020 won 2021 — which
inflates the headline "10 of the speculated candidates were eventually awarded"; those are *not*
same-year hits and I have excluded them.) I use **≈ 45%** for "the 2026 winner is on PRIO's list",
discounting the raw 55% for small sample and for one eccentric entry (the WTO).

## PRIO Director's 2026 shortlist (5 entries)

1. **Mykola Kuleba and Save the Children** — the impact of war on children
2. **Sudan's Emergency Response Rooms** — local civilian humanitarian networks
3. **The World Trade Organization** — multilateral trade as a foundation of interstate peace
4. **The Committee to Protect Journalists** — "reporting truth has increasingly become a matter of life and death"
5. **The International Court of Justice and International Criminal Court** — accountability and international law

Note that **two of the five (WTO, CPJ) fall into "Other organization"** on the Metaculus ballot.
So even a PRIO hit does not guarantee a named option.

## Trump specifically

Trump was odds-on before Christmas 2025 (55% implied) on the back of the Gaza framework, the
Abraham Accords, the "Board of Peace" charter and public nominations by leaders of Cambodia,
Israel and Pakistan. He has since collapsed to 1–4% across every market. The mechanism is
straightforward and is stated by Kalshi's own commentary: **the ongoing US war with Iran** —
a bombing campaign begun 2026-02-28, a naval blockade of Iranian ports, a collapsed ceasefire, and
the President publicly threatening on 2026-08-17 to "bomb the s--- out of" Oman, a US partner.
Separately, the Gaza deal's Phase 2 has stalled since January 2026 with no meaningful progress as
of July, and the Russo-Ukrainian "truces" of April and May 2026 were 32-hour and 3-day humanitarian
pauses, not settlements. **There is no live peacemaker narrative for 2026.**

## Decomposition

- **P(winner on PRIO's 2026 list) ≈ 0.45**: Sudan ERR 0.15 · ICJ/ICC 0.10 · Kuleba + Save the
  Children 0.08 · CPJ 0.07 · WTO 0.05.
- **P(not on PRIO's list) ≈ 0.55**: MSF 0.05 · Navalnaya 0.035 · UNRWA 0.025 · Pope 0.015 ·
  Trump 0.01 · everything else 0.415.

Then constrain to the base rate: **P(organisation first-listed) ≈ 30% historically**; the org-heavy
market board and PRIO list justify pushing it up, but not to the ~60% my raw component estimates
implied. The mechanism matters: **organisations are easier to name in advance than individuals**,
which is precisely why the visible boards over-weight them and why the favourite loses. Target
**P(org) ≈ 44%**.

## Dead ends and ruled-out hypotheses

- **Taking Kalshi's board at face value.** Its three org favourites sum to 50%, implying
  P(organisation) ≥ 50% against a 30% base rate on the first-listed rule. Thin ($800k) novelty
  markets over-price nameable favourites; that is the same bias that has made the Nobel favourite
  lose 8–9 years out of 10.
- **Normalising the bookmaker board.** The nine listed prices sum to 119.4% with **no "any other"
  price at all**, so normalisation would silently assign zero to the ~50% of probability that
  historically lands outside every published list. Used only for ordering, not levels.
- **Trump.** Not ruled out (1%), but the December-2025 case has been destroyed by the Iran war, the
  stalled Gaza Phase 2, and the Oman threats. Every market agrees; there is no contrarian case here,
  only a small tail for a sudden Iran settlement before 9 October.
- **Pope Leo XIV.** No pope has ever won the Peace Prize — not even John Paul II. Perennial
  speculation that has never converted in 125 years.
- **Reading the 2025 Machado award as evidence the Committee rewards US-aligned figures.** It is
  one data point and cuts both ways; Machado's dedication of the prize to Trump did not make Trump
  a likelier 2026 laureate — his odds fell afterwards.
- Searched for Sentinel Team, Good Judgment Open and INFER Nobel questions: **nothing found**.
