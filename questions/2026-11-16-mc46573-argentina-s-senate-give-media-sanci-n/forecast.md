## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-16

### What resolves YES

YES needs three things to happen in the Senate session of Thursday 26 Nov 2026 (it counts if the session runs past midnight into 27 Nov). First, the session has to take place. Second, the chamber must approve, both *en general* and *en particular*, a bill that suspends or eliminates the PASO for **all** national offices up for election in 2027. Third, that approval must give the bill media sanción, sending it to Deputies. Each of these outcomes resolves NO: no session, the bill not brought to the floor, a rejection, approval *en general* only, a text that makes the primaries optional, or a suspension covering only some offices, for example the presidential category alone.

The vote arithmetic: the Senate has 72 seats and the bill needs an absolute majority of 37. LLA holds 21 seats and the Peronist bloc of about 21 rejects the bill unanimously. The latest counts are 31–32 votes secured (La Nación and Infobae, 15 Nov), so the government is **5–6 votes short** with 10 days to go. There is also a step before the floor: the committee report (dictamen) needs 10 of the 19 committee signatures on 17 Nov. If it is signed then, the gap to 26 Nov is 9 days, which clears the usual 7-day waiting period before a floor vote.

### Outside view

No clean reference class fits. The nearest one is contested bills under Milei that were short of a majority about 10 days before a target session and had been stalled for 6 months. Those mostly slip to extraordinary sessions or get diluted, rather than passing on the first floor date: about 10–20 %, a judgement from memory and not a counted base rate. There is one precedent in the other direction. The 2025 suspension passed 43–20–6, but Peronist governors supported it then, and the Peronist bloc opposes it now.

### Inside view

- **Pro-YES evidence.** The government has accelerated. A committee meeting is called, a floor target is set, governors' deals are reported (Pullaro, Zdero, Santa Cruz, Neuquén), and Santilli says "las voluntades están". The government has closed similar gaps before. Diluting the text from elimination to suspension is designed to bring in UCR senators.
- **Against.** Every independent count is 5–6 votes short. Bullrich, the LLA bloc leader, repeatedly says the votes are not there, and Letra P reports that Bullrich and Menem have stepped back from Santilli's vote search. PRO doubts it will sign a dictamen if the text changes at the last minute. The bill has stalled since May and has missed earlier targets in September. The government has openly named December extraordinary sessions as a fallback, which lowers the cost of slipping past 26 Nov.
- **Resolution risk.** Reports say the new text may suspend the PASO only for the presidential race. If that is the text that passes, the question resolves NO. The same risk applies if the bill is diluted into optional PASO.
- **Combination.** I put dictamen on 17 Nov at about 55 %. Given a dictamen, I put the bill reaching the floor on 26 Nov and passing in full at about 25 %, and the approved text qualifying (all offices) at about 75 %. That gives about 10 %.

### What would change my mind

- **The 17 Nov committee meeting.** A dictamen signed with UCR signatures and a text covering all categories would move the forecast toward 25–35 %. Another cuarto intermedio, a pause in committee debate, would put it at or below 3 %.
- **Scope of the final text.** If the text suspends the PASO only for the presidential race, even a Senate win resolves NO.
