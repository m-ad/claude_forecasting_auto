## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-19

### What resolves YES

The question resolves to the first number on the "Wheat" line of the WEEKLY HIGHLIGHTS in the USDA FAS Weekly Export Sales report scheduled for Thursday 29 October 2026, which covers the week ending about 22 October 2026. That number is the total 2026/27 net sales for all wheat classes combined, in metric tons (MT). It excludes exports, single classes and cumulative totals. A net reduction resolves negative. If the release is delayed, for example by a government shutdown, the question waits for the report USDA designates as the 29 October release. The question's range is 0–900,000 MT, and both bounds are open.

### Outside view

The reference class is U.S. weekly wheat net sales in the early-autumn weeks of the current marketing year. Weeks reported for 2026/27 (2026 dates):

| Week ending | MT |
|---|---|
| Aug 6 | 255,900 |
| Aug 13 | 393,700 |
| ~Sep 24 | ~289,500 |
| Oct 1 | 451,600 |
| Oct 8 | 319,900 |

The Oct 1 figure was 68% above the four-week average, which puts that average at about 269,000 MT. The recent level is therefore about 270,000–350,000 MT. Week-to-week moves are commonly ±40–50%. In past years, late-October weeks have typically fallen between 200,000 and 600,000 MT. Net-negative weeks are very rare this early in the marketing year.

### Inside view

- **Lower:** sales for 2026/27 are running about 31% behind last year. Hard red winter (HRW) supply is tight after drought. USDA cut its 2026/27 export forecast by 25 million bushels to 750 million bushels in the October Wheat Outlook [3]. December SRW (soft red winter) futures fell about 7.6% in a month on ceasefire hopes, which can pause buyers.
- **Higher:** Black Sea disruption continues. Russian ports reportedly stay closed into 2027, and Russian export forecasts have been cut [4]. That gives U.S. origin some room in Asia, as the Philippines, South Korea and Japan buying in the Oct 1 week shows [2]. Large tender bookings can lift a single week above 500,000 MT.

These roughly balance. I centre the forecast at about 335,000 MT, a little above the four-week average, and skew it right because bunched buying produces large positive surprises more often than large negative ones. The tails are wide because I could not see the week ending Oct 15 (the Oct 22 report) or the PDF text of the latest report.

### What would change my mind

- A large flash sale or tender win in the week of 16–22 Oct, such as Egypt, Algeria or Japan/Korea tenders awarding U.S. wheat, would push the result above 500,000 MT.
- A confirmed Black Sea ceasefire that reopens Russian and Ukrainian ports would shift buyers away and pull the figure towards 150,000–250,000 MT.
- The Oct 22 report (week ending Oct 15) is the strongest single signal I have not seen.
