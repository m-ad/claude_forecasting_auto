## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-17

### What resolves YES

YES only if, by 28 Nov 2026 23:59 UTC, Nigerian federal or Rivers State authorities (NNRA, the police and so on) have publicly stated that at least one of the two Ir-192 sources is back under authority or licensee control. The statement must appear on the NNRA news page, or a credible outlet must attribute it to those authorities. A recovery of projectors "intact" counts. A statement from the licensee alone does not count. Neither does recovery of only empty projectors or the container, an arrest, or "located/traced". A recovery that is never publicised resolves NO.

The timeline: the theft was reported in early November, the NNSC (National Nuclear Security Committee) met on 9 Nov, and the release came out on 11 Nov. Today is 17 Nov, which leaves 11 days. On 17 Nov the NNRA news page lists this item 2nd of 16, below a newer, unrelated Gombe item. There is no recovery notice [1][2]. As of today the question would resolve NO.

### Outside view

**Reference class:** stolen industrial-radiography sources.

- **Recovery rate.** The IAEA's Incident and Trafficking Database (ITDB) says about one third of stolen sources over 10 years were never recovered, and that transport thefts are "more often than not" unrecovered [4].
- **Speed.** When cameras are recovered, it is usually within 1–6 days. Examples: Tulsa, Mexico 2015 and 2022, Indonesia 2024 [5][6]. A minority take weeks to months: Veracruz 2021 took about a month, Iraq 2015–16 about 3 months [5][7].
- **Conditioning on what we know.** Nothing was recovered in the first ~1–2 weeks. That removes most of the fast-recovery mass, so the chance of recovery in the next 11 days is roughly 20–30%.
- **Publicity.** Recovery then has to be publicised by an authority. The NNRA posts irregularly and with lags. I put the chance of a qualifying public statement at about 65–75%.
- **Nigerian precedent.** It is poor. A 2003 oil-sector source was still missing in 2008 [8].

Outside view: about 15–20%.

### Inside view

- **Points to NO.** This was a theft from a storage pit of six projectors plus a transit container. That looks like organised or scrap-oriented theft, perhaps for the depleted-uranium shielding' scrap value. It is not a vehicle theft where the camera gets dumped, so retrieval is less likely to be quick. The NNSC only "agreed modalities" for recovery [2].
- **Points to YES.** An unverified OtownGist social-media snippet claims the projectors were recovered "fully intact". I could not corroborate it, and OtownGist's own article does not carry the claim [3]. It is worth a few points, because recoveries in Nigeria often surface first in local media.
- **Bottom line.** 6 projectors give several chances to find at least one of the two loaded ones. A police parade of suspects with recovered items would count if the reporting says the projectors were intact.

Net: 16%.

### Evidence

- **NNRA release.** Six projectors (2 with Ir-192) and 1 transit container were taken from the storage pit. The theft was reported to the IAEA through the ITDB and the Unified System for Information Exchange in Incidents and Emergencies (USIE). The NNSC met on 9 Nov. No recovery, suspects or operator name [2] (fetched 2026-11-17).
- **NNRA news page (17 Nov).** No follow-up item. A newer, unrelated item sits above the breach item [1].
- **Searches.** 13 of mine plus 11 by scouts found no report of a recovery or an arrest, and no market [all].

### What would change my mind

The main thing is a credible Nigerian outlet quoting police or the NNRA on recovering the projectors intact. I could not verify the OtownGist claim; if it held up, the probability would go above 80%. In the other direction, an NNRA statement that the search is ongoing late in the window would push the forecast toward 5%.
