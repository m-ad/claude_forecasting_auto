## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-17

### What resolves YES

YES only if Sen. Dan S. Sullivan, his campaign or an authorised spokesperson publicly says he has lost, or that Peltola has won, by 23:59 UTC on 27 Nov 2026 (about 14:59 Alaska time that day, Thanksgiving Day). Statements that votes are still being counted, that the campaign is waiting for certification, or "if these results hold" do not count. A concession later followed by a recount request or an election contest still counts. Timeline arithmetic: the official ranked-choice (RCV) tabulation is on Wed 18 Nov, 9 days before the deadline. The certification target is Wed 25 Nov, 2 days before it. The campaign said on 16 Nov that it will not concede before certification, which leaves a window of about 2 days if that line holds and certification stays on schedule. Count as of 16 Nov [1]: Peltola 49.98%, Sullivan 45.93% of first choices. The remaining ~4.1% (Heikes 2.1%, Dan J. Sullivan 1.6%, write-ins 0.3%) would have to break about 99–1 for Sullivan to win, so Peltola wins the RCV count almost certainly.

### Outside view

Reference class: U.S. Senate incumbents who clearly lost after a slow count. Begich conceded on 17 Nov 2014, Nelson on 18 Nov 2018 after a recount, Casey on 21 Nov 2024, and Hyde-Smith on 11 Nov 2026. In Alaska, the Trump-backed Tshibaka conceded on 23 Nov 2022, the day of the RCV tabulation, even though she had earlier hinted at a contest [7]. Almost all of these conceded within about 3 weeks. Base rate for conceding within 24 days of election day: about 85–90% (n ≈ 6–8, recalled cases checked in one search; small sample).

### Inside view

- **The campaign says it will wait for certification (−20 pp).** On 16 Nov, spokesman Nate Adams said "we will let that process run its course until every legal vote is counted and certified" [2][3]. Certification is targeted for 25 Nov, but Alaska's targets have slipped before (2024: 30 Nov). If certification slips past 27 Nov and he keeps to this line, the question resolves NO.
- **National pressure against certification (−10 pp).** Trump has called the race "rigged" [5]. DOJ is appealing its suit to block certification, Gov. Dunleavy has hinted he might not certify [6], and the "ballot-stacking" story gives Sullivan grounds to keep a contest open (the contest deadline is 5 Dec). A senator who wants to stay on Trump's good side has reasons to hold off.
- **Signals that he will accept the loss (+5 pp).** On 1 Nov, on CNN, he pledged to accept the results [8]. The campaign said it would "have more to say by the end of this week," which falls after the 18 Nov tabulation [4]. The margin is far too large for a recount. Alaskan precedent (Tshibaka, Begich) is to concede on tabulation or certification day.

Net: about 62%.

### Evidence

- Peltola 49.98% vs Sullivan 45.93%; AP estimates 98% counted (16 Nov) [1].
- Sullivan's campaign will not concede until certification; more to say by the end of the week (16–17 Nov) [2][3][4].
- Kalshi: Democrats 99.6¢ to win the Alaska seat (13 Nov) [9].
- Dunleavy cast doubt on certification (29 Oct). Experts say the Division of Elections director certifies, not the governor [6].
