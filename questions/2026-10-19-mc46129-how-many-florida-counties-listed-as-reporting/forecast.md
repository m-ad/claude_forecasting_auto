### What resolves YES

This resolves to N in the sentence "In 2026, X cases of locally acquired dengue and Y mosquito pools have been reported from N counties". The sentence comes from the "Dengue Cases Acquired in Florida" section of the most recent weekly FDOH (Florida Department of Health) Arbovirus Surveillance Report dated on or before 2026-10-30. That should be the Week 42 report (Oct 18–24). If publication lags, it could be the Week 41 report (Oct 11–17). N counts counties with either a local human case or a positive mosquito pool. It is cumulative for the year, so it cannot fall below the current 8. The range is 8–14 with a closed lower bound and an open upper bound (>14).

### Outside view

The latest value is 8 counties, in the Week 40 report covering Oct 4–10 [1]. The 2026 path so far: 4 counties at W33 (mid-August) [3], 6 at W35–W37, 7 at about W38, 8 at W39 and 8 at W40 [1][2]. That is +4 counties in 7 weeks, about 0.55 new counties a week, or about 1.1 expected over the two remaining reports (W41, W42). The count was flat from W39 to W40, and the last first-local-case county announcement I found is Broward on Oct 5 [4][5]. For comparison, the 2024 season ended with 93 local cases in total [2]. 2026 is far larger (368 cases by Oct 10), so earlier years give little guide to how far the outbreak spreads.

### Inside view

The 8 counties are Hillsborough, Pinellas, Pasco, Polk, Orange, Miami-Dade, Palm Beach and Broward ("Broward is the eighth Florida county") [4][6]. Transmission is still strong: 72 new local cases in W40, concentrated in Hillsborough [1]. Neighbouring Tampa Bay counties are plausible next additions (Manatee, Hernando, Sarasota). So are counties under mosquito-borne illness alerts or advisories (Alachua, Duval, Brevard, Martin, Manatee) [1]. Late-October cooling and declining rainfall slow *Aedes* transmission, but reports lag onset by 1–3 weeks. I found no news of a ninth county as of 2026-10-19. I shade the expected number of additions to about 0.8–0.9, below the raw trend of 1.1, because W40 was flat and the reporting window is short (possibly only one more report, if W41 is the one in force on Oct 30).
