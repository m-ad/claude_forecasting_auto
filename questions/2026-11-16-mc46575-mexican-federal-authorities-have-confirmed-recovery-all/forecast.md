## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-16

### What resolves YES

YES only if, by 23:59 on 2026-11-27 Mexico City time (UTC−6), a Mexican **federal** body (CNSNS, CNPC, ININ, FGR, SSPC, Guardia Nacional, Presidency…) publicly confirms that **all nine** looted packages (3 Tc-99m generators, 4 I-131, 1 Tl-201, 1 Sm-153), or all their radioactive material, have been located and secured. Separate statements add up; an unqualified "the stolen material was recovered" counts. Partial recovery, statements that the material has decayed or is no longer risky, and state-only announcements (Tlaxcala, Veracruz governors) do **not** count. Arithmetic: 0 of 9 packages confirmed recovered as of the latest coverage I found (Nov 11–13). That leaves 9 to go in the 11.7 days left, after 9 days with none found.

### Outside view

Mexican stolen-source cases usually end in recovery: about 22 of 33 sources stolen in a decade were recovered, and CNSNS counted 12 of 14 in 2018. The well-known cases (Co-60 in 2013, found in 2–8 days; Ir-192 in 2014 and 2021) were **single** sources taken with a stolen vehicle and usually dumped within about a week. This case differs: nine boxes were looted by several residents during a rapiña (crowd looting of a crashed vehicle), and none had turned up after nine days. Recovering *all* of them, with a federal confirmation, is a much stricter event. Base rate for the reference class "single source recovered within ~3 weeks": about 60–70%. For "multi-item looting, all items recovered, nothing found by day 9": I estimate 15–25% (a judgement call; no such data exist).

### Inside view

- No box had been reported recovered as of Nov 11–13. Searches by me and two scouts (21 in total) found no coverage after Nov 13 reporting any hallazgo (find) [1–5].
- Nov 11: the FGR (federal prosecutor's office) identified a probable responsible person and cited "muchas inconsistencias" in the chronology and the chain of custody [4]. An arrest could produce a bulk recovery, the main path to YES (+3 pp).
- Nov 13: the FGR concluded the cargo was "material de uso médico y no una fuente radiactiva", and the alert is to be reclassified, possibly withdrawn [5]. Together with fast decay (I-131 half-life ~8 days, Mo-99 ~66 h), this points to a quiet wind-down rather than a formal "all nine secured" communiqué (−5 pp).
- Several looters → items scattered. Even if some are found, confirming all nine is unlikely (−15 pp from the base rate).

Net: about 8%, IQR 5–13%.

### Evidence

- Excélsior / Plano Informativo, Nov 8: CNPC alert in 7 states; condition of the containers unknown [2][3].

- SENER 2013 communiqué: CNSNS confirmed recovery of the Hueypoxtla Co-60 source two days after the theft. This is the template for a YES statement [6].

### What would change my mind

An arrest of the identified suspect, followed by a CNSNS/FGR statement that "el material fue recuperado" with no mention of missing items: that alone would resolve YES. The opposite case: a federal note withdrawing the alert because the material has decayed, with no recovery stated. That makes NO near-certain.
