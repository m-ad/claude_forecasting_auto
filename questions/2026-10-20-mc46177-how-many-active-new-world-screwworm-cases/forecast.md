## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-20

### What resolves YES

The question resolves to a number: the national "Casos activos" headline total (New World screwworm / gusano barrenador del ganado, all species and states) on Senasica's Power BI dashboard, taken from the report whose "Informe actualizado al:" date is the latest on or before 31 October 2026. In practice that is the report of about 26–31 October, roughly two weeks after the latest known value. A case counts as active from diagnosis and becomes inactive after a 15-day revisit finds no new wounds or larvae. If the dashboard is down, credible media quoting Senasica for that report are used. Both bounds are open, so values below 1,200 or above 2,800 are possible.

### Outside view

The reference class is the series itself. Since about May 2026 the national active count has stayed in a band of roughly 1,700–2,100 and tended to revert to the middle rather than trend: 1,706 (11 May), ~2,072 (late May), 1,696–1,841 (mid/late July), 1,963 (18 Aug), 1,905 (29 Aug), 1,880 (15 Sep), ~1,900 (11 Oct), 1,968 (14–16 Oct), 1,976 (17 Oct). Over 2–3 weeks the count typically moves ±50–150 (about ±3–8%), with occasional swings of ~250. The one large break was the holiday drop of December 2025 to January 2026 (1,145 → 492, −57%), which happened in midwinter, not October. Last autumn the count rose from 941 (21 Nov 2025) to 1,145 (10 Dec 2025), so late October shows no seasonal collapse. Prior: centred near 1,950, with a two-week standard deviation of about 100–120.

### Inside view

- **Latest level:** 1,976 at the 17 October cutoff, 1,968 in the 14–16 October report, and 2,025 quoted for epidemiological week 41. One Guanajuato outlet's 2,148 for 15 October looks like an outlier.
- **Northern surge:** Chihuahua went from 85 active (mid-September) to 135 (6 Oct), 174 (14 Oct) and 176 (17 Oct), driven by rain and humidity. Sonora reached 106 active, seventh nationally. These states are still rising while the south has been flat, which is mild upward pressure.
- **Offsetting factors:** cooler weather in the north from late October usually slows fly activity. Sterile-fly releases (aerial dispersal over Chihuahua and Sonora) and the 15-day reclassification cycle keep the total bounded. These argue against a breakout above about 2,200.
- **Data noise:** outlets give different numbers for the same week (for example 1,963 vs 1,905 in late August, and 1,968 / 2,025 / 2,148 in mid-October). The dashboard may be revised or updated more than once a week. The tails are widened for this.

**Net:** median 1,980, a little above 1,968 for the mild northern upward pressure. The IQR is ±~85 and the 5–95% range is about 1,730–2,240. Tails extend beyond that for reporting artefacts.

### What would change my mind

- A week-42 report (around 23–24 October) well away from ~1,970. A print above 2,100 would move the median up by about 80, and one below 1,850 would move it down.
- Evidence of a cold snap or a large sterile-fly effect sharply reducing northern detections, or a reporting change on the dashboard (a reclassification sweep) that would make the count jump.
