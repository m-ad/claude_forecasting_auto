## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-16

### What resolves YES

The question resolves to the number of posts the Trump's Truth archive lists on its search page with the date filter set to 2026-11-26 to 2026-11-26, in America/New_York time (00:00–23:59 ET, Thanksgiving Day). Originals, quote posts and reblogs each count as one, and deleted posts still count. The scale is 0–150 with an open upper bound. This search-listing count overrides the stats chart. The question writer's headline average (40 posts a day over the last 30 days) is a **mean** inflated by bursts: Nov 3 alone had 190 posts. The same search method, run today for single days, gives the typical day: Nov 8: 8, Nov 10: 9, Nov 11: 5, Nov 12: 12, Nov 13: 4, Nov 14: 17 posts. Nov 15 had 7 posts by about 20:10 ET, so that day was not complete. On this listing, Thanksgiving 2025 (Nov 27) had **5** posts [1–3].

### Outside view

Reference class: single-day counts in the archive. The 2026 mean is about 19–24 posts a day [4,5]. The daily distribution is strongly right-skewed: most days fall between 3 and 20, and roughly one day in 10–15 brings a spree of 50–190 posts (the WSJ counted 44 overnight spates of 12 or more posts in about 22 months [6]). Holiday days I checked in the archive: Thanksgiving 2025: 5, New Year's Day 2026: 3, July 4 2026: 19, Christmas 2025: 126 (a spree) [3]. The holiday median is about 12, with a fat upper tail.

### Inside view

- Since the midterms, posting has fallen back to about 4–17 posts a day; the median of the last 6 complete days is about 8.5. This moves the centre down from the year-long ~20 to about 10.
- Thanksgiving 2025, the closest analogue, had 5 posts: a midday post plus a late-night series of 3. That points lower, but Christmas 2025 shows a holiday can also produce a 100+ spree.
- The Epstein storyline (House vote forced by the Nov 11 discharge petition) could provoke posting, but no vote is scheduled on Thanksgiving. I add only tail mass for it.
- Result: median 9, IQR 5–16. About 10% of the mass sits above 33 and 5% above 58, for a spree, and about 5% at 0–1.
