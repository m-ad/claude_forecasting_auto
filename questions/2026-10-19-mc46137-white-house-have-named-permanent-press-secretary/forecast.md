## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-10-19

### What resolves YES

YES if, by 23:59 ET on 30 October 2026, Trump or the White House has publicly named someone other than Katie Zacharia as the (permanent) White House Press Secretary, and the announcement has not been withdrawn. The announcement can be a Truth Social post, an official statement, or a named official's confirmation reported by credible outlets. An "acting" or "interim" designation does not count. Neither does a Principal Deputy (e.g. Anna Kelly) handling briefings without the full title. The person need not have started: "slated to hold" counts. Check against Wikipedia's "Incumbent" field, which read "Vacant since October 16, 2026" when accessed on 2026-10-19 [1]. Window left: about 11 days (19–30 October).

### Outside view

Base rate: the time it takes to fill this vacancy.
- **This administration, Leavitt to Zacharia:** about six weeks. Leavitt left on 27 August 2026, and Zacharia was announced on 9 October [1][5]. The search reportedly stalled several times as candidates failed vetting.
- **Trump's first term:** successors were often named within days. Grisham was named in June 2019 on the day Sanders' exit took effect [8]. Sanders was announced on the day Spicer resigned (July 2017) [9].

Pooled, that gives roughly 25–35% that a successor is named within two weeks of a vacancy. That rate is for vacancies with a ready internal successor or a planned handover. It drops when the previous search went badly.

### Inside view

Points against a quick appointment:
- Two sources familiar with Trump's thinking told NBC that he is **unlikely to name a new press secretary anytime soon** (reported 16–17 October) [3][4].
- The last outside hire blew up after five days over vetting. That argues for a slower, more careful search, not a snap pick.
- The prior search took six weeks.
- Principal Deputy Anna Kelly can keep briefing without the title, which takes pressure off. Under the fine print, that resolves NO.
- I found no report from 17–19 October of an imminent pick [2][6].

Points for a quick appointment:
- Trump is impulsive and likes to "win the news cycle." Promoting Kelly, the Kalshi favourite already vetted inside the building [3][7], would be fast and low-risk.
- The midterms (3 November) give a reason to have someone at the podium.

Net: about 20%.

### Evidence

- 2026-10-16: Zacharia was ousted after five days, with no successor indicated [2][5].
- 2026-10-16/17: NBC, citing two sources, reported that Trump is unlikely to name a new press secretary soon. Names in circulation: Anna Kelly, Scott Jennings, Margo Martin, Steven Cheung, Maria Bartiromo, Hogan Gidley, Harrison Fields, plus self-nominations from Laura Loomer and George Santos [3][4].
- 2026-10-19: Wikipedia lists the office as vacant since 16 October, with no acting press secretary named [1].
- 2026-10-19: Searches found no report of a named successor [2][6].
