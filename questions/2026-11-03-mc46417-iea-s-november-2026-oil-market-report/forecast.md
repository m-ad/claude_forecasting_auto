## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-03

### What resolves YES

The question resolves to the full-year 2026 annual-average y/y change in world oil demand, as stated in the demand overview/highlights of the IEA Oil Market Report published 13 Nov 2026, in mb/d to one decimal (e.g. "falls by 2.7 mb/d" resolves to -2.7). The first-published figure governs. Last published value: October OMR (14 Oct 2026): -2.6 mb/d (102.4 mb/d; 2025 base about 104.99). Path of the same figure by edition: Jun -1.1, Jul -1.0, Aug -1.6, Sep -2.5, Oct -2.6. Arithmetic: one edition (Oct → Nov), 30 days apart. The gap to the nearest round values is 0.1 mb/d, the size of the last revision. The headline is rounded to one decimal, so the outcome is effectively discrete in 0.1 steps. The range is -4 to -1 with open bounds.

### Outside view

Outside crises the IEA moves annual growth by 0.1–0.3 mb/d per month (per the question text). In 2026 the revisions were -0.1 (Jul), -0.6 (Aug), -0.9 (Sep) and -0.1 (Oct). The last step decelerated sharply, and the 4Q26 view was left "largely unchanged". The single best predictor is the last published value, with a small drift in the direction of recent revisions and fat tails because of the crisis. This is a reference class of n≈5 recent monthly revisions, not a formal base rate.

### Inside view

- Direction for a further cut: the Strait of Hormuz is still effectively closed (about 3 transits per day against about 85 before the war). The IRGC reportedly re-declared closure on 26 Oct. Houthi strikes hit Saudi infrastructure. Brent ran $109–117 for most of 20–29 Oct, above the $102–105 of the October OMR's cut-off. The October OMR assumed "hostilities gradually ease". Lagged Sept/Oct data on diesel (availability -1.7 mb/d y/y in 3Q) could come in weaker.
- Direction for no cut or a smaller one: the Oct revision was only -0.1 mb/d. On 2 Nov Brent settled at $104.77 (-7.6%) on Qatar-mediated talks, which is about where it stood when the October report was written. Indian Sept petrol (+7.2%) and diesel (+4.9%) demand was firm. A deal would mostly lift 2027, not 2026, with only about 6 weeks of the year left at the time of the report. Markets price only 5–8% odds of Hormuz normalisation by Dec/Jan.
- Baseline revisions to 2025 are a two-way risk of about ±0.1–0.2.
- Net: a modest lean to a further small cut, median -2.7, with probability mass on -2.6 (about 20%). Tails are wide on both sides (reopening or collapse in the data).
- Assumption tested by the disconfirmation search: that the revision stays small. The scouts found no sign of a shock large enough for a ±0.5 move, since even October's record attacks produced only -0.1.
- Adjustment: -0.1 mb/d on the median from the last value, one event (the Hormuz re-escalation and price run-up in October against a decelerating revision pace).
