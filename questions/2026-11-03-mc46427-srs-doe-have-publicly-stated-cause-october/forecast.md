## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-03

### What resolves YES

YES if, between 28 Oct and 11:59 p.m. ET on 13 Nov 2026, SRS, DOE, SRNL, or their contractors (SRNS, the SRNL operator), or an on-the-record spokesperson for any of them, publicly names what started the 28 Oct SRNL fire: an electrical fault, equipment failure, chemical reaction, hot work and so on. A cause stated as "probable", "suspected" or "preliminary" counts. The statement can be in an SRS news release, the public.srs.gov banner, a DOE or SRNL web page, or credible reporting that attributes it to one of those entities. "Under investigation" does not count, and neither does naming only the room or the time. Leaks, unnamed sources and advocacy groups do not count. The window runs 16 days from the fire, and 10 days remain as of 3 Nov. On 3 Nov the SRS news-release page held five releases dated 28 Oct or later. None named a cause: the three on 28 Oct covered the emergency, and the two on 2 Nov were unrelated [1]. The nearest public forum, the Citizens Advisory Board meeting on 17 Nov, falls after the deadline [6].

### Outside view

The reference class is significant fires or emergency events at DOE nuclear sites, and how often an official cause is stated within about two weeks. No systematic dataset exists. The examples found point to a rate of roughly 10–20 %:
- The Los Alamos (LANL) PF-4 glovebox fire of 15 Jun 2026 still had no public cause after 24 days [7].
- Earlier fires at LANL, Idaho National Laboratory (INL) and SRS stayed "under investigation" in the initial coverage.
- Minor fires sometimes get a quick informal cause, such as the Hanford 2023 candle fire. That came through Defense Nuclear Facilities Safety Board (DNFSB) staff reports, which are not an SRS or DOE statement.
- Formal DOE accident investigations take months.

Base rate: about 15 %.

### Inside view

- **Down:** six days after the fire, the only official line is DOE Environmental Management's "investigation into the cause is underway" (Monte Volk, to the Post and Courier, 29 Oct) [3]. Since then SRS has posted only unrelated releases [1]. The site has been tight-lipped: it has not even named the room or the materials involved. Its credibility problem over the retracted "unfiltered ground-level release" notice makes it likelier to wait for a formal finding than to float a cause.
- **Up:** press and watchdog pressure (WRDW, the Post and Courier, SRS Watch) and the radiological-release controversy could push out a preliminary statement. A one-room lab fire can have an obvious mechanism, such as an equipment or experiment ignition. An LLM resolver might also count loosely worded reporting.
- **Net:** about 12 %.

### Evidence

- 28 Oct: SRS declared a Site Area Emergency (17:07 or 17:39 ET; sources differ) for a single-room fire at SRNL. The emergency was terminated at 21:45. One employee had minor injuries [2][4].
- 29 Oct: SRS said there had been no radiological release, reversing the "unfiltered ground-level release" notice given to the state [2].
- 29 Oct: WRDW reported that DOE and SRS had not released "how the fire began" and that investigations "typically take weeks or months" [2].
- 29 Oct: DOE-EM said a cause investigation is underway [3].
- 3 Nov: the SRS news-release page carries nothing on a cause [1].
- No reporting found from 30 Oct to 3 Nov that names a cause, in searches by me and by three scouts (22 queries in all).

### What would change my mind

- An SRS or DOE statement on the room or the materials. If they start disclosing details, a cause may follow.
- A DNFSB weekly site report giving a preliminary cause that local media then attribute to the contractor. That would push the forecast up sharply.
