### Outside view

Reference class: Alaska statewide first-choice leaders on election night versus ~day 10 (2020 Senate, 2022 House/Senate, 2024 House, Aug 2026 primary; n = 5). The election-night leader never lost the lead to a rival from the other side except in the 2022 Senate (R-vs-R, moderate overtook). In every case the late count moved the margin 1–3 points (2020: ~10) toward the Democratic/moderate candidate. So the day-10 leader ≈ the final-electorate leader, with a modest D tilt. Poll-based prior: Peltola leads the average by ~3 points (PollCheck, 27 Oct: 47.2 vs 44.1), a lead of about 0.6 standard deviations given ~4–5 pt error on a thin-polled state.

### Inside view

Start: Peltola +2 to +3 first-choice (late polls: Siena 48–46, ASR 48–46, PPP 48–47, InsiderAdvantage 48–43 four-way; counter: Trafalgar and co/efficient had Sullivan +1/+2 in September). Two adjustments, each from a distinct event:
1. Late-ballot shift toward Peltola (Aug primary: margin 5.3 → 8.1 pts after late counting; 2024: Peltola gained ~1–2 pts of margin): pushes up. Polls already try to measure the whole electorate, so the tilt only partly adds to the poll lead (the Nov 13 snapshot is slightly more Democratic than election night but still not the full count); I applied only a small net plus (~+3 pp).
2. Historical Alaska polling error against Republicans (Sullivan beat 2020 polls by ~8) pushes down.
Net expected margin ≈ Peltola +1.5, sd ≈ 4.5 → P(Peltola leads) ≈ 63 %; markets at ~70–74 % for her winning (undated snapshots; RCV win probability, closely correlated). Blend ≈ 66–67 %. Sullivan Jr. and Heikes: well under 1 % combined to lead, with a small tie/other allowance.
