## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-03

### What resolves YES
A D.C. federal grand jury must return, and the return must be publicly disclosed, an indictment (or superseding indictment) of Comey covering the October 2016 disclosure conduct charged in the information in US v. Comey, 1:26-cr-00229 (D.D.C.), by 23:59 UTC on 2026-11-13. An indictment under a different D.C. case number counts if it charges the same disclosure conduct (including as part of a broader conspiracy or scheme). An indictment limited to unrelated or 2017-or-later conduct does not count, and a sealed indictment does not count. The existing information (filed Oct 5, unsealed Oct 26) is not a grand jury indictment. Dates: information filed 2026-10-05; hearing 2026-10-30 with no ruling; "next Thursday" = 2026-11-05 is reported as technically the last day to seek an indictment under the Speedy Trial Act before the information is dismissed (8 days before the question's end date). As of the last reporting I found (Oct 30), no indictment had been returned. There is a risk that an indictment built on post-October 2016 conduct (DOJ mentioned April 2017 evidence) is judged "unrelated". The conspiracy charge makes that less likely, but it is not zero.

### Outside view
No clean reference class exists. Two partial ones:
- (a) When DOJ publicly says it is presenting to a grand jury, the usual base rate of indictment is above 95 % (federal no-bills are very rare).
- (b) In the current environment in D.C., politically sensitive Trump-DOJ presentments are refused often. Reported cases: the Feb 2026 unanimous refusal to indict six Democratic lawmakers, the Oct 27 2026 refusal to indict Cassidy Hutchinson, and at least 8–9 felony no-bills since the 2025 takeover of D.C. law enforcement. I cannot count the successful presentments, so I cannot give a rate. My own rough guess is that 40–60 % of the high-profile politically charged presentments fail in D.C. (speculation).
- Comey himself has been indicted once on two of three counts (E.D. Va., Sept 2025), which was later dismissed on the Halligan appointment ground, and once over the "86 47" post.

### Inside view
For YES:
- DOJ told the court on Oct 30 that it is presenting evidence to a grand jury.
- It went to unusual lengths (a sealed information on the last day of limitations).
- It has a hard deadline around Nov 5.
- Judge McFadden did not bar a grand jury attempt and seems inclined to let it proceed.
- Prosecutors can re-present to another grand jury if one refuses.
- The Nov 13 window leaves about 10 days.

For NO:
- Grand jury refusals: the judge himself said an indictment is "no sure thing".
- The evidence looks thin and old. Two earlier investigative teams (Durham in 2018; the 2020 probe) reportedly declined to charge.
- The prosecutor said "they may indict, they may not".
- The Speedy Trial clock may be tolled while the defense motion is pending, which would relieve the Nov 5 deadline and let DOJ slip past Nov 13 (I could not verify the clock arithmetic; a "30-day pause from Oct 9" is unverified).
- A no-bill would be publicly reported through the new chief-judge notice policy, and re-presentment takes more days.
- An indictment limited to 2017 conduct might not count.
- Nothing in the reporting through Oct 30 shows an indictment is imminent.

Net: I start from about 60–65 % (DOJ intent plus ordinary grand jury base rate) and cut roughly 25 pp for the D.C. refusal pattern, the weak evidence and the timing and resolution-scope risks (see the research log's `adjustments`). That gives about 35 %.

### Evidence
- Information (two counts, §793(d) and conspiracy) filed under seal 2026-10-05, unsealed 2026-10-26; Comey did not waive indictment [1][4][5].
- Oct 30 hearing: DOJ lawyer Dustin Farahnak said the department is presenting evidence to a grand jury and "they may indict, they may not indict"; McFadden did not rule, joked that "ham sandwiches are harder to indict these days", and said he would be "extremely frustrated" by a dawn-raid arrest [1][2].
- Grand jury in D.C. refused to indict Cassidy Hutchinson on 2026-10-27 [3].
- Aug 31 2026: grand jury subpoena to Daniel Richman in a renewed leak probe [6].
- No report of an indictment dated Oct 31–Nov 3 found in about 30 searches (absence is weak evidence for the last days).

### What would change my mind
- A report that a grand jury had returned a true bill (or a no-bill) on or after Nov 1 would settle most of the uncertainty. A no-bill with no re-presentment would put me near 5 %.
- A ruling that the Speedy Trial clock is tolled and that DOJ has until after Nov 13 would push me to about 20 %.
- A report of a second grand jury being used or of strong new witness testimony would push me to about 50 %.
