## v1 — Initial forecast — 2026-11-16

### What resolves YES

YES only if the Dewan Rakyat holds at least one recorded division ("belah bahagi", ayes and noes counted) on the Supply Bill 2027 between 08:00 MYT on 26 Nov and 04:00 MYT on 27 Nov 2026 (00:00–20:00 UTC on 26 Nov). It can be on a committee-stage allocation (including a motion to cut a ministry's budget) or on the third reading. The official Hansard decides, with press reports as the fallback. A voice vote does not count, a division on any other business does not count, and a division on any other day does not count. That includes the third reading if it slips to 30 Nov or later. Arithmetic: a division needs 15 MPs to stand. The opposition outside the confidence and supply agreement (CSA) has roughly 222 − 147 = 75 MPs, so it can easily force one if it wants to. The question is whether it wants to, on that day.

### Outside view

Reference class: the decisive late stages (committee-stage allocations and third reading) of Anwar-government budgets, 2023–2026. The third readings of Budget 2023, 2024 (27 Nov 2023), 2025 (3 Dec 2024) and 2026 (1 Dec 2025) all passed by **voice vote**: 0 of 4 [1][4][5][6]. I found no committee-stage ministry division under this government either. Budget 2026's allocations passed by voice vote [8]. The only recent budget division came at the policy stage (4 Nov 2025, 120–67–35) [1]. The opposite regime is the Budget 2021 cycle under a thin-majority government, when 9 of 27 ministries went to a division [9]. This parliament has a comfortable majority, so it fits the voice-vote regime. Laplace's rule on 0/4 gives about 17%. Committee-stage divisions also look rare, so the per-sitting base rate is about 8–12%.

### Inside view

- **−3 pp:** this year the opposition did not even call a division at the policy stage (29 Oct), the stage where it called one last year [2][3]. It is also fragmented. Hamzah Zainudin now leads Parti Wawasan Negara, and Bersatu split over the CSA [10]. Every Budget 2027 committee allocation reported so far passed by voice vote [3][6].
- **−2 pp:** timing. On 13 Nov, 16 ministries were still to wind up [6]. Budget 2026's committee stage was scheduled to end 27 Nov 2025 but the third reading slipped to 1 Dec [5]. If 26 Nov runs over, the decisive vote moves out of the window.
- **+3 pp:** motive. The opposition can put the 13 CSA signatories on the record against Perikatan Nasional (PN) on the final day. Early-election talk and the UMNO–DAP friction after Loke resigned on 10 Nov [7] make that a plausible move for PN. The question writer also highlights this possibility, which is mild evidence it is being discussed.

Net is about 8%. The IQR (interquartile range) reflects uncertainty about PN's tactics.

### Evidence

- Budget 2027 passed the policy stage by voice vote on 29 Oct 2026. No opposition MP rose to call a division [2][3].
- 13 Nov 2026: four more ministries were approved and 16 remain. The committee stage runs to 26 Nov, and approvals have been by majority voice vote [6].
- Budget 2026: a division at the policy stage, but the 1 Dec 2025 third reading went by voice vote [1][5].
- Budget 2025, third reading on 3 Dec 2024: voice vote [4]. Budget 2024, on 27 Nov 2023: voice vote [11].
- 15 Nov 2026: there are calls to dissolve Parliament, but analysts expect the budget to pass first [7].

### What would change my mind

The biggest risk to this forecast is a PN statement before 26 Nov that it will force a bloc vote to expose the CSA MPs, or any committee-stage division between 16 and 25 Nov. Either would push the forecast to 30–50%. A break in the government coalition, with UMNO MPs wavering, would raise it further.
